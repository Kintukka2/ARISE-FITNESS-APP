# Action roguelites: how repeated runs pay

Research for ARISE. Topic: how Hades, Hades II, Dead Cells, Rogue Legacy 2, Children of Morta, Risk of Rain 2, Enter the Gungeon, Moonlighter and Cult of the Lamb make each run different, preview and offer rewards, pay for failure, keep difficulty ahead of an invested player, and tell a story across runs.

**Method and confidence.** I ran 22 web searches and about 40 page fetches. The shared search budget for the session ran out before the 30-search target, so some details come from fewer sources than planned. Fandom wikis (Hades, Dead Cells, Rogue Legacy, Moonlighter) were blocked by Cloudflare, so exact rules come from wiki.gg (Dead Cells, Risk of Rain 2, Enter the Gungeon), Fextralife (Hades) and guide sites, plus developer interviews where they could be reached. Anything marked **[unconfirmed]** comes from memory or from a single low-reliability source; check it before relying on it.

---

## 1. The games

### Hades (Supergiant, 2020)

**Loop.** Fight through four regions of chambers. Each cleared chamber offers one to three exits, and each exit shows the reward the next chamber pays. Death returns you to the House of Hades, where meta currencies buy permanent upgrades and the story moves on.

**Boons and the gods.** Nine Olympians give Boons (Aphrodite, Ares, Artemis, Athena, Demeter, Dionysus, Hermes, Poseidon, Zeus), and Chaos gives Boons through Chaos Gates. A Boon fills one slot (Attack, Special, Cast, Dash, Call), and a newer Boon for that slot replaces the old one. Passive Boons take no slot. An offer is one Boon from three choices; the Approval Process Pact condition removes one choice per rank. Rarities are Common, Rare, Epic and Heroic. The Mirror's Olympian Favor adds +1% Rare chance per rank (40 ranks), Gods' Pride +1% Epic (20 ranks), and Gods' Legacy +1% Duo or Legendary (10 ranks). A Pom of Power raises one Boon's level, and a Daedalus Hammer changes the weapon itself.

**Duo and Legendary Boons.** A Duo is offered only if you hold specific prerequisite Boons from each of its two gods (Prima counts 28 Duos). Each god has one Legendary, two for Hermes and Poseidon, each with named prerequisites. Fully Loaded (Artemis) needs two of Support Fire, Pressure Points and Exit Wounds. Winter Harvest (Demeter) needs two of Killing Freeze, Ravenous Will and Arctic Blast. Meeting the prerequisites makes the Boon eligible, not guaranteed.
*Why it works:* prerequisites turn a pile of picks into a plan. From the third or fourth pick the player is steering toward a combination, and each door choice is read against that goal. The Codex shows the prerequisites, so the plan is legible.

**Door previews.** Every exit shows its reward glyph inside a laurel. A **gold laurel** is power for this run: Olympian Boons, Pom, Hammer, Centaur Heart, Charon's Obols. A **blue laurel** is a meta currency: Darkness, Chthonic Keys, Gemstones, Nectar. A skull under the glyph marks a harder encounter (Trial of the Gods, a mini-boss, double Darkness). Rerolls stay within the same laurel colour. The Mirror's Fated Authority grants rerolls of a chamber's reward, and its alternate, Fated Persuasion, rerolls Boon and Well offers.
*Why it works:* every fork asks whether you want to be stronger now or stronger forever, and the player answers it knowingly. The laurel colour makes the meta economy a deliberate choice, not a side effect.

**Chaos Gates.** Optional side doors that cost Life to enter (the Cosmic Egg keepsake removes the cost; exact HP cost **[unconfirmed]**). A Chaos Boon pairs a curse with a blessing. The curse runs for a set number of encounters (Atrophic: −25 to −49 max Life for the next 3–4 encounters), then the blessing switches on for the rest of the run. Chaos blessings stack.
*Why it works:* paying now for later is a different shape of decision from "pick one of three". The player has to judge whether the next three rooms can be survived weakened.

**Well of Charon.** A shop of temporary buffs bought with Obols, with durations counted in encounters: Eye of Lamia costs 25 for 3 encounters, Nail of Talos 65 for 6, Nemesis Crest 60 for 6. One appears after each boss. Light of Ixion guarantees a Chaos Gate ahead. The Loyalty Card cuts Well prices by 20%.

**Pact of Punishment (Heat).** Opens after the first escape. Fifteen conditions, 63 Heat in total outside Hell Mode:

| Condition | Ranks × Heat | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hard Labor | 5 × 1 | Foes +20% damage per rank |
| Lasting Consequences | 4 × 1 | Healing −25% per rank |
| Convenience Fee | 2 × 1 | Prices +40% per rank |
| Jury Summons | 3 × 1 | +20% foes per rank |
| Extreme Measures | 1/2/2 | Each rank gives one boss new techniques |
| Calisthenics Program | 2 × 1 | Foes +15% HP per rank |
| Benefits Package | 2/3 | Armoured foes +1 perk per rank |
| Middle Management | 2 × 2 | Mini-bosses get an elite or a distraction |
| Underworld Customs | 2 × 2 | Give up a Boon at each region exit |
| Forced Overtime | 2 × 3 | Foes +20% speed per rank |
| Heightened Security | 1 × 1 | Traps +400% damage |
| Routine Inspection | 4 × 2 | Switches off 3 Mirror talents per rank, from the bottom |
| Damage Control | 2 × 1 | Foes block one hit per rank |
| Approval Process | 2/3 | One fewer Boon choice per rank |
| Tight Deadline | 2/3 | A region timer, 9:00 down to 7:00 |

**Bounties.** Heat is tracked per weapon. Each boss pays a bounty the first time you beat it with that weapon at a new Heat: the Furies pay Titan Blood, the Bone Hydra a Diamond, Theseus and Asterius Ambrosia, Hades more Titan Blood. A region already cleared at an unbeaten Heat pays a little Darkness instead. You must escape at Heat N−1 with a weapon before Heat N bounties count for it, so skipping ahead forfeits the levels in between. Sources say bounties run to Heat 20 **[unconfirmed]**.
*Why it works:* the player chooses which pain to take, so difficulty follows taste. Tying bounties to the weapon makes the endgame a reason to play all six weapons, and the "N−1 first" rule paces the climb.

**Mirror of Night.** Twelve talent pairs bought with Darkness, the later rows also with Chthonic Keys. You run one side of each pair, and swapping is free between runs. Examples: Death Defiance has 3 ranks at 30, 500 and 1000 Darkness and revives you at 50% Life once per rank; its alternate, Stubborn Defiance, costs 600 and revives at 30% once per chamber. Thick Skin is +5 Life per rank over 10 ranks. Dark Foresight is +2% chance of gold-laurel rewards per rank. The Routine Inspection condition can switch talents off, so meta power is itself something you can wager.

**Keepsakes.** Each character gives one for your first Nectar gift. You equip one at a time and can swap between runs, or at each region's start with the Keepsake Collection upgrade. A keepsake levels by use: rank 2 after 25 cleared encounters, rank 3 after 50 more. God keepsakes make the next Boon come from that god at +10–20% chance of Rare or better.
*Why it works:* a keepsake lets the player steer the random offers without removing them.

**Weapon aspects.** Six weapons with four aspects each, bought and levelled with Titan Blood. One guide says about 5 Titan Blood maxes the base aspect and the others cost about 15 each **[unconfirmed]**. The hidden fourth aspects unlock through story.

**Story advancing on death.** Kasavin: "It was an explicit goal of our early development, to take the pain out of dying and having to restart", and "The story would advance when you died." The game checks conditions after each run, such as which boss killed you, what you are carrying, or your Life when you met a character. It picks from pre-written events, each with a priority and conditions, and plays each line once. Low-priority small talk is skipped when a high-priority event is waiting. Kasavin on bosses: "you've seen them 50 times already", so why shouldn't characters "acknowledge and be self aware of the cycle". The true ending needs 10 escapes **[unconfirmed, widely reported]**.
*Why it works:* a failed run still produces something new, a conversation, and the novelty is spread over dozens of hours.

**God Mode.** An optional toggle: 20% damage resistance, +2% for each death, up to 80%. Kasavin said an easy mode was rejected because dying and looping is the game. "What if we just make you a little bit tougher?"

**What players criticise** (from community threads; not individually sourced): Heat bounties across 6 weapons × 4 aspects become a chore. Extreme Measures bosses and Tight Deadline are disliked. Duo hunting depends on RNG. Late Heat runs feel samey once a few builds dominate. The 10-escape ending reads as padding to some.

### Hades II (Early Access 2024, 1.0 2025)

**Loop.** As Hades, with two routes (the Underworld and the Surface) and a hub at the Crossroads. Resources gathered with tools during runs feed the Cauldron's Incantations, which unlock systems and hub changes.

**Arcana cards** (in place of the Mirror). 25 cards on a 5×5 grid. Each costs 0–5 Grasp. Grasp starts at 10 and rises to 30 with Psyche. Unlocking a card reveals its neighbours, and there are 6 presets. Six cards cost nothing but switch on only when a condition about the rest of the layout holds (Early Access figures, 1.0 values may differ):
- Messenger: three active cards share one cost.
- Centaur: one active card of each cost from 1 to 5.
- Queen: no more than two active cards share a cost.
- Seer: Fates, Champions and Boatman are active.
- Divinity: a full row that does not include it is active.
- Judgment: no more than three cards are active, and it switches on three inactive cards after each Guardian.

Examples of paid cards: Eternity (4 Grasp) starts a run with a Death Defiance. Boatman (5) starts with 200 gold. Fates (3) gives a Change of Fate, a reroll of a location's reward. Excellence (5) makes Boons 30% likely to be Rare.
*Why it works:* a budget turns meta upgrades into a loadout that gets re-planned. The conditional zero-cost cards reward composition over raw power. Presets make switching cheap.

**Oath of the Unseen (Fear).** Heat's successor: about 16–17 Vows of up to three ranks each. Guides cite a maximum of 67 Fear, though one source computes about 55, so treat the figure as unsettled **[unconfirmed]**. Fear gates the endgame rewards; guides describe Testaments with Fear floors, but the details conflict **[unconfirmed]**.

**Chaos.** The curse lasts a set number of encounters, and then the blessing is permanent.

**Fated List of Minor Prophecies.** It arrives through story. You brew the Fated Intervention incantation (1 Silver, 2 Ash, 2 Moly), which does nothing visible until you die, and then Moros brings the list. Rewards (Bones, Nectar, Ashes) are claimed by hand.

**Scale.** About 30,000 voice lines, roughly 50% more than Hades.

**Criticism.** Kasavin on the ending: "it was both not what they were expecting, and not what they were hoping for." Community complaints: resource gathering and menu sprawl **[community]**.

### Dead Cells (Motion Twin / Evil Empire, 2018)

**Loop.** A side-scrolling run through branching biomes, where kills drop Cells and occasionally blueprints. Between biomes you spend Cells at the Collector on permanent unlocks, or lose them when you die.

**Biome route choice.** After each biome you choose among two or three exits (for example Promenade, Toxic Sewers or Arboretum after the Prisoners' Quarters). Runes found in runs open other routes for good, and Boss Cells open extra doors (BC1 adds Prison Depths to the Ancient Sewers).

**Cells.** Dropped by kills, chests and bosses. All are lost on death unless spent at the Collector at a biome transition. One exception, the Residual Cells bag after the Hand of the King, holds what you carried plus 40. Cells buy blueprint unlocks and the Blacksmith's quality odds.

**Blueprints.** When a level is generated, each blueprint drop is assigned to a specific enemy at a rarity tier: Always 100%, Common 10%, Uncommon 1.7%, Rare 0.4%, Legendary 0.03%. A biome drops at most one outfit and one other blueprint from enemies. A blueprint is lost if you die before delivering it to the Collector; since v2.3 delivery happens automatically at transitions. 42 blueprints are hidden in secret areas.
*Why it works:* a blueprint picked up mid-run makes the rest of the run matter more ("I have to get this home"). It is the same tension as ARISE's banked loot.

**Scrolls of Power.** Each is +1 to one of three stats: Brutality, Tactics or Survival. Two-stat scrolls give a choice of two. Four Fragments make one scroll, and Fragments appear only from BC3. Items scale with the stat of their colour, so each scroll is a commitment to a build.

**Cursed chests.** Opening one curses you for 10 kills (5 with Acceptance, 15 with Alienation), and any damage while cursed kills you. Each holds a Scroll of Power, a gem and a colourless item one gear level up (++ at BC 0–2, S at BC 3–5). Spawn rates: 1% in the Prisoners' Quarters, 10% in most early biomes, guaranteed in Ossuary-tier biomes.
*Why it works:* the price is stated, temporary, and paid in skill. The player chooses when to open it, for example right before an easy corridor.

**Timed doors.** 2 minutes (Promenade, Toxic Sewers), 8 minutes (Ramparts, Ossuary), 15 minutes (from Black Bridge), 19:30 and 26:00 for later biomes. Each holds 20 cells, gold and an item altar offering 1 of 3, and some hold a blueprint. Time in shops, transitions and lore rooms doesn't count. The altar's minimum quality rises with Boss Cells: + at BC 0–1, ++ at BC 2–3, S at BC 4–5.
*Why it works:* a counterweight to exploring everything. Speed and completeness are separate strategies, and each pays.

**Boss Stem Cells.** BC N is earned by beating a final boss at BC N−1. Each level changes the early boss phases. Healing is the main lever: at BC1 fountains appear at every other passage, at BC2 none (one flask refill per passage instead), at BC3 three refills per run, at BC4 none. BC4 adds enemy teleports and longer detection, and BC5 adds Malaise. Pay rises too: cell drops ×2 at BC2–3 and ×3 at BC4–5, item levels +1 and +3, a higher Forge cap, new doors and BC-only blueprints.
*Why it works:* each level opens content, not just bigger numbers.

**Forge rework from feedback.** The first Forge raised specific weapons' stats, and players said it funnelled everyone into a few weapons ("something was really off"). It now raises the odds of better-quality finds overall. Bénard estimated that 40–50% of Early Access changes came from player feedback.

**Criticism** (community). Each Boss Cell needs a full clear. The early biomes repeat. Every unlock dilutes the item pool, so builds get less consistent the more you unlock (Custom Mode was later added to prune it **[unconfirmed]**). Malaise is unpopular.

### Rogue Legacy 2 (Cellar Door, 2022)

**Loop.** Choose an heir, run a procedural castle, and die. Gold buys permanent manor upgrades and equipment.

**Heirs and traits.** You pick 1 of 3 heirs, each with a class and traits. Many traits carry a gold bonus from +25% to +200% on everything earned that run. Teddy Lee: "Make everything have a trade-off!" One-Hit Wonder (one hit point) "with its massive gold bonus … is the most popular Trait in the game." With Pacifist you can't attack, "but mannnn, can you make a lot of gold!"
*Why it works:* a bad draw becomes a wager the player can choose. The handicap is the price and the gold is stated up front.

**The Architect.** Locks the castle's layout so the next run meets the same map, for a cut of the gold found. Rogue Legacy 1 took 40% **[unconfirmed]**. It trades map variety for knowledge, at a stated price.

**Scars of Erebus.** Fixed, hand-built challenges with no randomness, combat or timed, graded Bronze, Silver or Gold against a par shown on the challenge since v0.5.0. They pay souls.

**New Game+ (Threads of Fate).** Each Thread adds +1% enemy damage and +1.5% enemy HP. Burdens are chosen modifiers with weights: the budget grows by 2 a Thread up to NG+10 and by 1 a Thread to NG+25, capped at 35 **[single source]**.

**Criticism.** "Extremely grind-heavy" even for the genre.

### Children of Morta (Dead Mage, 2019)

**Loop.** A family of playable Bergsons clears procedural dungeons. Back at the house, story vignettes play and coins buy upgrades for the whole family.

**Mechanics.** Each character levels separately. House upgrades bought with coins apply to everyone, and every few levels a character unlocks a passive shared with the whole family. Corruption, or fatigue, builds on a character you run repeatedly and lowers their health until you play someone else **[exact numbers unconfirmed]**. A narrator tells the story at home between runs, and side events in dungeons change the house.
*Why it works:* shared upgrades mean a new character is never far behind; one run made new characters viable for a reviewer. Fatigue pushes you to rotate.

**Criticism.** Being forced to rotate characters; the narrative called "sloppy" by one reviewer; grind.

### Risk of Rain 2 (Hopoo, 2020)

**Loop.** Loot chests with gold, charge a teleporter, fight its boss and move to the next stage, while difficulty climbs with the clock.

**Difficulty = time.** From the wiki:
- `coeff = (playerFactor + minutes × 0.0506 × difficultyValue × players^0.2) × 1.15^stagesCompleted`.
- difficultyValue is 1 on Drizzle, 2 on Rainstorm, 3 on Monsoon.
- Enemy level = 1 + (coeff − playerFactor) / 0.33. Each level is +30% HP and +20% damage.
- Chest cost = base × coeff^1.25. A Small Chest is $25 on stage 1.
- The timer shows named bands: Easy, Normal, Hard, Very Hard, Insane, Impossible, I SEE YOU, I'M COMING FOR YOU, HAHAHAHA.

The designers wanted to force the player to keep moving. They tried scaling on kill speed, but it removed the time pressure.
*Why it works:* every chest you open costs time, and time is difficulty, so looting and leaving are a trade the player feels each minute.

**Items and stacking.** A Small Chest gives 79.2% common, 19.8% uncommon and 0.99% legendary items. Stacking is linear (Soldier's Syringe 1 + 0.15n), hyperbolic (Tougher Times 1 − 1/(1 + 0.15n), 60% at 10 stacks) or exponential (Shaped Glass 2^n damage). Hyperbolic stacking is the safety valve that keeps defensive items from reaching 100%.

**Lunar items and coins.** Each kill has a 0.5% chance to drop a Lunar Coin, halved after each drop that run, and coins persist between runs. Lunar items have big upsides with built-in downsides; Shaped Glass doubles damage and halves HP.

**Artifacts.** Run-wide rule toggles unlocked by codes found in the world plus a trial fight. Command lets you pick each item. Sacrifice removes chests and makes monsters drop items. Swarms doubles spawns at half HP. Glass gives 500% damage at 10% HP. Kin limits each stage to one monster type. Honor makes every enemy elite. Evolution gives monsters items between stages.

**Eclipse.** A fixed ladder of 8 cumulative levels per survivor; you advance one by winning. Level 1: start at 50% health. 2: teleporter radius −50%. 3: lethal fall damage. 4: enemy speed +40%. 5: healing −50%. 6: enemy gold −20%. 7: enemy cooldowns −50%. 8: permanent damage. It pays nothing beyond a gold icon.

**Criticism** (community). Late runs become unreadable. Time pressure punishes exploring. Eclipse is a long grind with no payoff.

### Enter the Gungeon (Dodge Roll, 2016)

**Loop.** Fight down five chambers to kill your character's Past. Bosses pay Hegemony Credits, which unlock items into future runs at the Breach.

**Shrines** (cost, then effect):
- Ammo: Curse +3.5, refills all ammo.
- Fallen Angel: −1 heart container and Curse +1.5, for +25% damage.
- Blood: −1 container, then draining marked foes heals you.
- Companion: −1 container, for a companion item.
- Challenge: survive three waves, for a chest that may be a mimic.
- Cleansing: 5 money per Curse point, for Curse 0.
- Dice: random, one good and one bad effect.
- Peace: your current gun, to heal one heart.
- Y.V.: 10 money, +10 per use, for extra shots.

**Curse.** Each point raises the chance an enemy spawns Jammed (stronger): 1% at 1–2, 2% at 3–4, 5% at 5–6, 10% at 7–8, 25% at 9, 50% at 10+. At 10 the unkillable Lord of the Jammed hunts you. Many strong items carry Curse.
*Why it works:* power has a visible price that accumulates, so every pickup is weighed against a meter.

**Synergies.** Item pairs with hidden combined effects; the wiki lists more than 185. When you hold one half, a blue arrow over your head and an outline in the Ammonomicon point to the partner.

**Secret floors.** The Oubliette (a trapdoor in the first floor), the Abbey of the True Gun and Bullet Hell.

**The Breach.** Bosses pay 1–3, 1–4 or 2–5 credits by floor group, doubled when you take no damage. Buying everything costs 1,804. NPCs rescued from cells inside runs move into the Breach and open shops and services: Ox and Cadence open the main unlock shop, the Sorceress sells Blessed Mode, Daisuke Challenge Mode. Repairing elevators with credits opens shortcuts to deeper floors, and shortcut runs pay only 1–2 credits a boss. Each character's story ends in their Past, a scripted chamber.
*Why it works:* the hub fills with people you rescued, so your history shows on it.

**Criticism** (community). Hard and opaque; synergies are discovered on a wiki; credits come slowly.

### Moonlighter (Digital Sun, 2018)

**Loop.** At night you raid one of four dungeons; by day you sell the loot in your shop. Profits upgrade the town (blacksmith, potion maker) and your gear.

**Dual-use loot.** Every item can be sold or kept for crafting and upgrades, so a haul is a portfolio decision. You set prices yourself and customers react to each price with a face, from delighted (too cheap) through content to angry (too dear). A notebook records the price bands you have learnt, and overselling one item lowers its demand. Other rules **[unconfirmed]**:
- Dying loses most of the bag.
- The Merchant Pendant leaves a dungeon with your loot for a fee.
- Some loot carries bag "curses" that constrain where it can sit or destroy neighbours.
*Why it works:* the dungeon run continues into the shop. A rare drop isn't simply good: you must choose between money now and gear later.

**Criticism** **[community]**. Pricing becomes rote once the notebook is full; the dungeons are short; the economy is easily solved. OpenCritic: 78% recommend.

### Cult of the Lamb (Massive Monster, 2022)

**Loop.** Short roguelite "crusades" through four regions yield resources and recruits, and the base between them needs tending. Followers need food, shelter and sanitation. Neglect breeds dissent, and dissenters can leave with coins. Sermons and rituals raise faith, and followers can be sacrificed for power. Tarot cards pick (1 of 2) during crusades **[unconfirmed]**.
*Why it works:* each run is fed by the base and feeds it, so a short run still matters.
**Criticism.** Game Informer found the customisation hard to engage with. The Washington Post found combat a chaotic mess. Players report base chores interrupting the runs **[community]**.

## 2. Cross-cutting principles

1. **The reward is chosen, and shown before you commit.** Hades' door glyphs, Dead Cells' timed doors and cursed chests, Gungeon shrines and RL2's trait gold bonus all state the prize and the price first. Choice under stated terms is what makes a run's route a decision instead of a walk.

2. **Two kinds of reward at every fork: power now and power forever.** Hades makes this explicit with gold and blue laurels. Dead Cells does it with cells spent at the Collector against scrolls, Moonlighter with selling against crafting. A run is interesting when the player can trade one for the other.

3. **Builds come from small pieces with visible prerequisites.** Duo and Legendary Boons, Gungeon synergies (with the blue arrow), Dead Cells scroll colours and RoR2 stacking all reward planning across picks. The game shows what completes a combination, and the random offers supply when.

4. **Every power has a price, and the player chooses when to pay it.** Teddy Lee's "Make everything have a trade-off!" covers Chaos curses, cursed chests, Gungeon Curse, lunar items and gold-paying traits. The good ones make the price temporary, or something the player can plan around.

5. **Pressure against safe farming.** RoR2's clock, Dead Cells' timed doors and Hades' Tight Deadline make exploring cost something, so speed and thoroughness are two strategies, not one dominant routine.

6. **Failure pays something definite.** Hades keeps blue-laurel currencies on death and advances the story. Dead Cells keeps cells spent at checkpoints. Gungeon pays credits at each boss, not at the end of the run. RL2 converts every run's gold into the manor. What failure pays is stated and limited, so success still matters.

7. **Difficulty is bought with stated rewards, and often tied to a build.** Hades and Hades II pay bounties per weapon at each new Heat or Fear, and you must clear N−1 first. Dead Cells' Boss Cells open doors, blueprints and item tiers. RoR2's Eclipse is per survivor but pays almost nothing, and it is the most criticised of these ladders.
   - **Hades: à la carte.** You choose which pain, and the total is the score.
   - **RoR2 and Dead Cells: fixed ladders.** Every player shares the same steps.
   - **ARISE's Ascension** is a fixed ladder like Boss Cells, with Gladiator Manager's proof and Trial on top:
     - Every rung is calibrated, and each pays 10% more, a mark and a step past perfect.
     - It sends a Chosen out with a masterwork.
     - It lacks two things Heat has: a player cannot skip a rule they dislike, and nothing pays them to replay the ladder with a different build.

8. **The hub changes because of runs.** The House Contractor in Hades, the rescued NPCs in Gungeon's Breach, Children of Morta's house and Cult of the Lamb's base all change in response to deeds. Story delivered between runs is a reward, and Hades makes it reactive with priority-weighted, condition-gated lines, each played once.

9. **Unlocks should widen choice, not dilute it.** Dead Cells' growing item pool made builds less consistent. Hades steers with keepsakes and Codex prerequisites, RoR2 offers the Command artifact, and Hades II has Arcana presets.

10. **Mercy keeps the loop.** God Mode makes you "a little bit tougher" per death, and Gungeon's shortcuts start you deeper for lower pay. Neither makes enemies easier or removes the loop.

## 3. Ideas for ARISE

Ranked by how much they address ARISE's known weaknesses: the board flattening for an invested hunter from C-Rank to S-Rank, currency piling up at the top, shallow early turns, and Auto carrying most play. Each is checked against the constraints: turn-based and one-handed, offline, deterministic for a seed and a list of orders, playable by the brief, Auto and calibration, nothing that sells time, and nothing from Solo Leveling.

**What ARISE already does as well or better:**
- **Honest odds.** No game here tells you your odds. Hades shows the reward behind a door, never the risk. The brief's 24 simulated runs is a genuine advance; keep every idea below inside it.
- **Foe intents as promises.** These are stronger information than Hades' reaction telegraphs, and they are what makes a turn-based choice meaningful.
- **Ascension: the Trial, the Chosen in the world, masterworks.** Richer than RoR2's Eclipse, which pays nothing, and Dead Cells' Boss Cells, which have no named content. Marks are close to the Mirror of Night.
- **Blueprints are permanent and forged on demand.** This avoids Dead Cells' and Gungeon's pool dilution. Loot banked on the run and lost on a wipe already gives Dead Cells' "get it home" tension.
- **Weapon Familiarity.** It ranks up with use, as Hades keepsakes do (25, then 50 more encounters).
- **Fatigue.** It does Children of Morta's corruption job without forcing a character swap.
- **Map memory.** A map holds all day and the fog remembers it, which gives Rogue Legacy's Architect lock without the fee.

### 1. Path blessings with Duos and Capstones (from Hades' Boons, Duo and Legendary Boons) — large

- **What to build.** Replace the five flat shrine blessings (`BLESSINGS` in `data/nodes.ts`: +20% HP, +15% attack and so on) with blessings from the five Paths: Ruin, Bastion, Tempest, Arcana and Sight. About eight per Path, each in three stated tiers. They change how the hunter's skills behave rather than adding stats. Examples:
  - Ruin: Strike leaves Rend, so the next hit on that foe deals +15%.
  - Bastion: Guard also covers the weakest ally.
  - Sight: +20% damage against a foe whose intent targets you.
  - Tempest: the first skill each fight acts at +30 initiative.
- **How it is offered.** A shrine offers one of three from one Path, and the plan shows the Path before you step in (see #2).
- **Duos.** One for each pair of Paths (ten), offered only when the run holds a blessing from both.
- **Capstones.** Need two of three named blessings in one Path.
- **Deepen.** A rarer room that raises one blessing's tier, the Pom equivalent.
- **Steering.** The Path holding most of the hunter's tree points is offered more often, as god keepsakes steer Hades.
- **What it plugs into.** The shrine node, the run (`run.blessings`), and the fight through the same run modifiers Ascension rules use. `raid.systemChoice` takes a fixed rule (the hunter's main Path, then highest tier).
- **The decision it adds.** Runs differ in kind, not just in numbers. Early turns get texture, because blessings make target order and Guard timing matter against shown intents.
- **Constraints.** Offers are seeded from the run and the node and written by `raid.choose` before they play. The System's picks are deterministic, so the brief, Auto and calibration play them. Either keep each effect to one the auto policy already exploits, or teach `choose()` to read it. Either way, recalibrate.

### 2. Rewards shown on the plan (from Hades' door glyphs and the gold or blue laurel) — medium

- **What to build.** When a room comes out of the fog, its marker states what clearing it pays, in two colours:
  - **Power for this run:** a blessing's Path, Deepen, a Health Potion, mana.
  - **Pay at exit:** alloy of a stated grade, family parts, Shadow Essence, "blueprint 8%".
  - Caches and rests keep passing for Events, so the surprise layer stays.
- **What it plugs into.** Room content in `core/map.ts` and its glyphs in `NODE`. Pay is banked on the run and paid in `raid.finish` as now.
- **The decision it adds.** Every fork asks "stronger now or richer later". The road, a side branch and the Gatekeeper become priced options. This fulfils the third pillar, which a fight room currently fails ("Pays shards, sometimes gear").
- **Constraints.** Contents are drawn from the map seed. The System's road is unchanged; if power rooms fall on it, recalibrate.

### 3. Oaths: Ascension's rules taken one at a time below S-Rank, with bounties (from Hades' Pact and per-weapon bounties, RL2's Burden weights, RoR2's Artifacts) — medium

- **What to build.** From C-Rank, the brief offers Oaths drawn from rules the engine already plays: Thin Rest, Short-Handed, Heavy Legs, Wardens Wake, Crooked Luck, Hungry Dark, Never Alone, Worn Thin, By Hand, and steps of foe strength.
- **Weight and pay.** Each Oath has a weight of 1–3 and stated pay of +10% per weight. The brief's clear rate updates as Oaths are toggled.
- **Bounty.** The first clear of each grade at each new total weight pays a one-off (a blueprint of the grade, or alloy). As in Hades, weight N pays only after N−1 has been cleared.
- **Not a second ladder.** No proofs, marks or Trial. That is the complaint that moved Ascension to S-Rank, so Oaths are a per-Gate dial on the brief, not a ladder.
- **Optional, later.** Per-Path bounties at Ascension levels would give a reason to climb again with another build (the role Hades' per-weapon Heat plays).
- **What it plugs into.** `core/ascension.ts` rule hooks, and `run.oaths` kept like `run.ascension`.
- **The decision it adds.** It answers finding #1, an invested hunter outgrowing the board between C-Rank and S-Rank, with difficulty the player picks and is paid for. It also teaches Ascension's rules before Ascension opens.
- **Constraints.** Every Oath is already playable by the engine and the brief. `gatetune.ts` is unaffected, since Gates are tuned with no Oaths. The campaign needs a policy (take Oaths while the brief stays at 75% or better) and a rerun for the economy.

### 4. Scars: fixed-seed challenges graded by par turns (from RL2's Scars of Erebus and Gungeon's no-damage boss doubling) — medium

- **What to build.** A set of hand-built fights and three-room mini-Gates on fixed seeds, each with a stated loadout or the hunter's own. They are graded Bronze, Silver or Gold by turns taken and HP lost, and the bar for Bronze is the turn count Auto achieves on it.
- **Pay.** Essence or Ember once per medal.
- **What it plugs into.** The combat engine and a row in the Quests log. The result is settled in one engine call.
- **The decision it adds.** ARISE's determinism, a seed plus a list of orders, makes each Scar an exact puzzle with a known optimum, which no action roguelite can offer. It pays the player for out-playing Auto, which answers "Auto can carry most play" and the first pillar.
- **Constraints.** No calibration is needed (fixed content). A test checks each par is reachable. Small fatigue or none.

### 5. A step clock: the Gate stirs, and sealed caches (from RoR2's difficulty = time and Dead Cells' timed doors) — small to medium

- **What to build.** Count rooms entered; walking back stays free.
- **The clock.** Past the road's length plus a stated slack, foes grow +5% a room, and the plan shows "The Gate stirs 2/5".
- **The cache.** A sealed cache, marked with its count, opens only if reached within N rooms entered. It holds an altar offering 1 of 3, like Dead Cells' timed-door altar.
- **What it plugs into.** `run.steps`, a node flag in `core/map.ts`, and a run modifier the fight reads.
- **The decision it adds.** Clearing every room stops being free. Push or explore becomes a real trade, with the Every Room and Cartographer Secret Quests as its prize for thoroughness.
- **Constraints.** Steps are not real time, so nothing runs on a timer. The System's road is the shortest, so it never triggers the stir and never detours for the cache: calibration is unchanged.

### 6. Rifts and cursed caches (from Hades' Chaos Gates, Dead Cells' cursed chests and Gungeon's shrines) — small to medium

- **What to build.** A room that states a price now and a prize later. Examples:
  - "For the next 3 fights foes act first; then attack and magic +20% for the rest of the Gate."
  - "−1 Health Potion now; Shadows +15% for the Gate."
  - A cursed cache: a stated rich roll (a blueprint chance, a piece found whole). Until three fights are won, a hit for more than 25% of max HP voids the cache (it stays banked but marked).
- **The decision it adds.** A different shape of choice from "one of two blessings": the player judges the next stretch of the plan. That needs map knowledge, which the fog and the Survey make a resource.
- **Constraints.**
  - Terms are seeded and stated, and the choice is written by `raid.choose`.
  - The System declines Rifts, so the brief and calibration are untouched.
  - The Rift panel can run the projection both ways, "with: 71% · without: 78%", keeping honest odds at the point of choice.

### 7. Stairs as a checkpoint: secure or carry (from Dead Cells' Collector, and Hades keeping meta currency on death) — small

- **What to build.** At stairs down, the hunter may secure everything banked so far for a stated tithe (say 25%). What is secured is paid even if the party is wiped. Unsecured loot pays in full on exit.
- **What it plugs into.** `raid.secure`, written to the run and settled in `raid.finish`, which keeps the fifth pillar.
- **The decision it adds.** Deep Gates (two to four floors) get a mid-run choice about risk that today only the entrance offers.
- **Constraints.** Auto never secures, and the campaign measures the economic effect. Watch that it doesn't drain the sting a wipe is meant to carry; the 12-hour lockout stays.

### 8. Lines that notice (from Hades' priority-weighted, condition-gated dialogue) — medium (mostly writing)

- **What to build.** After each run, `flow.ts` may show one line from a pool, chosen by conditions and priority, each shown once.
  - **Conditions:** the run was carried out by a given family; the first Gatekeeper; a Shadow joined; an Association hunter fell; the third wipe on one Gate; a Rift taken.
  - **Speakers:** Ryn's lines stay on the Association's side and never touch the System. System notices use `ui/system.ts` templates.
  - **Story beats:** some of the 19 can also be reached through failure, such as "carried out by the same boss three times".
- **What it plugs into.** A lines table, and a set of lines seen added to `defaults()` with no migration.
- **The decision it adds.** None; it is a feeling. A wipe produces something new, which is Kasavin's "take the pain out of dying".
- **Constraints.** No engine or calibration impact. The writing follows VOICE.md, and `tests/voice.test.ts` checks what it can.

### 9. Gate omens on the daily board (from RL2's traits with a gold bonus) — small to medium

- **What to build.** Each board Gate carries 0–2 omens drawn from its seed. An omen is a rule with stated pay, from +25% to +100%. Examples: "Starved: no potions, +50%"; "Crowded: +1 foe a fight, +25%"; "Lightless: the Survey doesn't work, +25%".
- **The decision it adds.** Choosing a Gate from the board becomes a wager, the way choosing an heir is in RL2. It complements #3: omens are drawn, Oaths are chosen.
- **Constraints.** Every omen is a rule the engine plays, so the brief stays honest. Gates are tuned without omens. The campaign checks the pay.

### 10. Command: a budget for the Shadow squad, with formations (from Hades II's Arcana, Grasp and conditional cards) — medium

- **What to build.** The squad's slots become a Command budget that grows with rank and can be raised with Essence up to a cap. A Shadow costs by its rank (Bound 1 to Ascendant 4).
- **Formations.** Free bonuses that switch on when the squad meets a condition: three of one family; one of each family; no two of the same rank (Queen-style); an awakened Shadow leading (Seer-style).
- **Auto Deploy** optimises within the budget, and squads can be kept as presets.
- **What it plugs into.** `core/shadows.ts` squad slots. Secret Quests that grant slots grant Command instead.
- **The decision it adds.** A composition puzzle instead of "your five strongest", and an Essence sink at the top (finding #2).
- **Constraints.** Deterministic. The balance tools' `keptUpHunter` builds its squad through Auto Deploy, so recalibrate.

### 11. Burn or bank: materials usable in the run (from Moonlighter's dual-use loot) — small

- **What to build.** Materials found in the run can be spent from the bag before they reach the Forge. A mana core restores mana; a beast hide gives a fight's worth of Guard+. A Materials slot joins the bag's wheel (`bagFor`), with its engine call in `core/` like `raid.drinkPotion`.
- **The decision it adds.** Spend future gear to survive now, the choice Moonlighter's shop poses.
- **Constraints.** Written to the run. The System never burns, so the brief and calibration are untouched.

### 12. A hall that fills (from Gungeon's Breach rescues, Hades' House Contractor and Cult of the Lamb's base) — medium

- **What to build.** A rare event room holds an Association hunter trapped in the Gate. Free them (a stated fight) and they join the Association hall, opening a service paid in shards:
  - A reroller of one gear property, a sink DESIGN.md already names.
  - A cartographer selling Surveys at a discount.
  - A trainer who moves a Shadow toward its next rank for shards, the other named sink.
  - The board marks the Gate holding today's rescue.
- **What it plugs into.** The event room drawn from the map seed; the services are ordinary engine calls between runs.
- **The decision it adds.** The hub records what the hunter did, and shards gain lasting uses (finding #2).
- **Constraints.** Ryn and the hall stay ignorant of the System. The campaign must spend shards on the services to measure them.

### 13. Odds you can buy (from Dead Cells' Blacksmith and the Mirror's Olympian Favor and Gods' Pride) — small

- **What to build.** A capped shard sink that raises stated chances: the vault's blueprint from 12% toward 20%, the chance of a piece found whole, extraction odds +1% a step up to +10%. The Guide quotes each figure from the constants.
- **Why this form.** Dead Cells' feedback lesson: raising the odds of better finds is healthier than boosting one item, which funnels builds.
- **Constraints.** These are seeded rolls; rerun the campaign. Calibration is untouched, since its hunters carry typical gear.

### 14. Resolve, opt-in (from Hades' God Mode) — small

- **What to build.** An accessibility toggle, off by default. Each wipe adds +2% damage reduction, up to a stated cap, and the brief shows and plays it.
- **Why.** Kasavin's reasoning applies: keep the loop and make the hunter "a little bit tougher", rather than weakening foes.
- **Constraints.** The odds stay honest because the projection includes it. Calibration and the campaign run with it off.

**Order to build.** #2 first: it is cheap and makes #1, #5, #6 and #9 legible. Then #1 and #3, the two that change what a run is and who it challenges. #4 is the best fit for ARISE's deterministic engine and the clearest answer to Auto.
