# Action-RPG loot, crafting, build depth and endgame ladders: research for ARISE

Scope: Diablo II, III and IV, Path of Exile 1 and 2, Last Epoch, Grim Dawn, Torchlight, Minecraft Dungeons. Rules and numbers come from official patch notes, developer posts and interviews where I could reach them, and from Maxroll and other community wikis otherwise. **[unconfirmed]** marks anything I could not check against a source in this session. The session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so Torchlight, parts of Path of Exile's map system, Diablo III's Paragon and Diablo IV's Paragon boards rest on memory. They are flagged where they appear.

---

## 1. Per game

### Diablo II (2000; Resurrected 2021)

**Loop.** Clear acts on three difficulties, then repeat short "runs" (Mephisto, Pindleskin, the Cow Level) for random drops, stacking Magic Find to tilt the odds. Most of the endgame is trading for, and assembling, runewords.

**Runewords.** 33 runes, El to Zod, go into a **normal (white or grey) base** of an allowed type with **exactly** the listed sockets, **in the listed order**; wrong order sticks. Enigma (Jah·Ith·Ber, 3-socket armour) gives Teleport to every class; Insight (Ral·Tir·Tal·Sol, polearm) gives a Meditation aura, usually to the mercenary. The Horadric Cube upgrades runes: El–Ort take 3; Thul–Lem take 3 plus a gem; Pul–Cham 2 plus a gem (diablobytes). *Why it works:* the recipe is public and deterministic and the scarcity is all in the ingredients, so every high rune is legible (a Ber is a third of an Enigma) and low runes stay useful because the Cube walks them up. A runeword gives a capability no stat buys. High runes became the trade currency because they are homogeneous, scarce and useful.

**Magic Find.** Effective MF = MF × F / (MF + F), with F = 250 for uniques, 500 for sets and 600 for rares; magic items have no diminishing returns. At 300% raw MF, uniques see 136%; at 500%, 167%. Guides stop at about 250% and buy kill speed: a 200-MF Mephisto in 7 seconds out-finds a 300-MF one in 10 (diablobytes). The soft cap makes MF a gear-swap decision rather than a replacement for power.

**Cow Level and Uber content [unconfirmed details].** Wirt's Leg and a Town Portal tome cubed in town open the Cow Level after Baal dies on that difficulty; killing the Cow King locks the character out. Hell bosses' keys, then organs, open Uber Tristram and the Hellfire Torch. Selling Stones of Jordan to vendors summons "Diablo Clone": a server-wide currency sink with a spectacle attached.

**Criticised.** Bots and duping wrecked trade; runs repeat; Treasure Class rules are opaque; single-player high runes are close to unobtainable.

### Diablo III (2012; Reaper of Souls 2014)

**Loop.** Run Nephalem Rifts for keys and loot, then push timed Greater Rifts to level legendary gems and climb leaderboards. Each season restarts every character on a fresh ladder.

**Launch versus Loot 2.0.** At launch, drops were plentiful and mostly useless, and the real-money auction house became the real loot source; a widely cited critique called D3 "a frustration loop, temporarily alleviated by the Auction House". Loot 2.0 (patch 2.0.1, February 2014) was "Less is More": one projection put the yellow items seen at 83 instead of 275. "Smart drops" rolled stats for the finder's class (often cited as 85%; **[unconfirmed]**), and legendaries gained powers that change skills. The auction house closed on 18 March 2014. Josh Mosqueira's GDC 2015 lessons: randomness alone does not create emotion, so focus on the fantasy. In interviews he said "every time there's a drop, it has to have some utility" and "If you're just forcing players to vacuum stuff up, it's terrible."

**Greater Rifts.** Fill a kill bar, then kill the Rift Guardian, within 15 minutes. Monster health grows ×1.17 a level; damage ×1.13 to GR25, ×1.07 to GR70 and ×1.023 to GR150, so above GR70 health doubles every 4.5 tiers and damage every 30. The cap is 150, with "no plans to increase" it. A finish with 10–15 minutes left opens +3 tiers, and each death after the first adds 5 seconds to the respawn. Leaderboards hold the top 1,000 per class, and since Season 23 one per class set.

**Legendary gems** level only at the Guardian. The chance per attempt depends on GR level minus gem rank: 100% at +10, 90/80/70% at +9/+8/+7, 60% from +6 to 0, then 30/15/8/4/2% at −1 to −5, 1% to −15 and 0% below. A run gets 3 attempts, +1 with no deaths, +1 for gold paid to "empower" it. Gems rank to 150 and gain a second effect at 25 (Maxroll). *Why it works:* the ladder pays an item whose growth is tied to pushing *above* your comfort level, so it keeps feeding the build after gear is finished.

**Kanai's Cube (2.3).** A legendary is destroyed and its power extracted *at its maximum value* (5 Death's Breath plus one of each bounty material) into a permanent collection. Three slots take one weapon, one armour and one jewellery power, swapped freely. Law of Kulle rerolls a legendary (5 of each bounty material, 50 Forgotten Souls); Hope of Cain turns a level-70 rare into a random legendary *of that slot* (25 Death's Breath, 50 of each salvage material) (diablobytes). *Why it works:* a bad roll is never wasted, the collection feeds a completionist urge, and three constrained slots make combinations without unbounded stacking.

**Pylons and goblins.** At most 4 pylons a rift, each type once, spawn chance rising from about 1% to 100% at half progress; Power gives ×3.5 damage for 30 seconds. Treasure goblins are loot that runs for a portal. Both are readable spikes of fortune inside a run. **Paragon [unconfirmed details]:** uncapped levels after 70, each adding a point to the next of four categories in rotation; most stats cap at 50 points, the main stat never.

**Criticised.** Set dominance (six-piece multipliers in the thousands of percent leave about one viable build per set, rotated by season); the Paragon treadmill (at four digits, levels are invisible); number inflation that made single drops matter less.

### Diablo IV (2023 →)

**Loop.** Level to the cap, then cycle Helltide, Nightmare Dungeons, the Pit, boss ladders and seasonal activities. Each one feeds a different crafting system on your gear.

**Before Season 4.** Items carried four mostly weak or conditional affixes, so judging an upgrade meant reading every line. Legendaries were salvage for Aspects, and each imprint consumed the Aspect. The team called uniques "not exciting enough". Rod Fergusson said Destiny's loot cave "has to not exist" because it bypassed every other source.

**Season 4, "Loot Reborn" (May 2024; official notes).**
- **Fewer, stronger affixes:** legendaries 3, rares 2. Fewer drops, more of them worth having.
- **Greater Affixes:** 1.5×, on Ancestral items only, and not enchantable.
- **Codex of Power:** salvaging a legendary stores its Aspect *permanently*; a stronger copy upgrades it.
- **Tempering:** reusable manuals in six categories, two tempered affixes on Ancestral items, limited by Tempering Durability.
- **Masterworking:** fed by the Pit. Secondary sources give 12 ranks of about +5% to all affixes, with ranks 4/8/12 adding +25% to one *random* affix, and a reset to zero.
- **The Pit:** 200 tiers, 10 minutes, deaths cost 30/60/90 seconds. A boss kill with 4–6 minutes left opens +1 tier; 6 or more, +2.
- **Stated intent:** make an upgrade easy to spot, and move complexity "into Tempering and Masterworking".

**The correction (Season 11, patch 2.5.0, December 2025; official post).** The player now *chooses* the tempered affix, one per item, with charges restorable without limit. Masterworking raises Quality instead (to 20 at launch; Maxroll's 2026 guide lists 25 at +1% each), and at the top a Capstone turns one random affix into a Greater Affix, rerollable without losing Quality, for 100–200 Obducite plus 10,000,000 gold a reroll (a large gold sink). Base affixes go back up from 3 to 4. The developers said tempering's "brick early rather than later" intent had aged badly and bricking felt punishing, and that the Masterworking change "may have removed too much" randomness; they are weighing bringing back "hype".

**Sanctification (Season 11).** An opt-in final gamble: the item may gain a Greater Affix, an extra affix or a legendary power, or have an affix replaced. Afterwards it cannot be modified.

**Infernal Hordes (Season 5).** A compass of 8 tiers runs 5 to 10 waves. After each wave you take one of three Boon-and-Bane pairs, which stack. Burning Aether, spent on Spoils at the end (20, or 60 for the better ones), is *lost* if revives run out.

**Helltide (Season 4)** has a threat meter that climbs as you kill until you are Hell-Marked and ambushed, and three Baneful Hearts summon the Blood Maiden. **Paragon and Glyphs [unconfirmed details]:** grid boards with legendary nodes and glyph sockets; a glyph's bonus scales with attributes allocated in its radius, and glyphs level in Nightmare Dungeons and the Pit, which ties the ladder to the build.

**Criticised.** Season 4 tempering bricked items; Masterwork crits fell on random affixes; Sanctification is called "just another lottery"; activities pay a sameness of loot; each season's meta collapses onto a few builds.

### Path of Exile (2013 →) and Path of Exile 2 (2024 →)

**Loop.** Level through acts, then run the Atlas of maps, modifying each one with currency for risk and reward. Gear is crafted by spending currency items that are also the trade money. A new league with a new mechanic arrives roughly every 13 weeks **[cadence unconfirmed here]**.

**Currency as crafting (GGG's 2011 dev diary).** Gold needs forced sinks (repairs, death taxes), inflates, gets farmed and makes progress predictably linear. GGG chose barter items that are homogeneous, useful, scarce and easy to handle, each doing one thing to an item (reroll, upgrade rarity, add a mod), so "each currency item is its own sink": most trades end with the receiver using it on the spot. The sink is the crafting attempt itself, so it never stops mattering while there is an item to improve.

**Deterministic versus random: the Harvest manifesto (March 2021).** Harvest let players add or remove specific mods, and they made "ridiculous items". GGG wrote that "a critical aspect of item acquisition is that it is through random (rather than deterministic) means", that "obtaining perfect items is ideally close to impossible", and that crafting should show "significant diminishing returns"; a designer wanted to keep "the feeling of closing your eyes and Exalting an item". The reaction ran to about 400 mostly hostile forum pages: it "takes away the only viable way to get decent items" for players without hours to trade. GGG's case for randomness rests largely on a trade economy; players' case for determinism rests on respect for their time.

**Maps and the Atlas [partly unconfirmed].** Maps run tiers 1–16 (PoE1). Rarity sets the modifier count (rare maps up to 6, corrupted more). Each modifier adds item quantity, rarity and pack size, and the map shows those totals *before you enter*. Some mods are lethal to some builds (reflect, no regeneration), so players reroll or skip them. That is risk chosen per map against your own build. The Atlas passive tree specialises a player's maps toward the league mechanics they want to farm, which makes it a target-farming layer.

**PoE2 waystones [unconfirmed details].** Waystones replace maps. A death in a waystone map ends that map, so bonus loot from mods is bought with real risk. Towers with Precursor Tablets modify the maps around them.

**Delve.** Sulphite fuels a crawler, its cost per segment growing with depth to 1,100; darkness deals 2% of Life and ES per stack per second. Depth raises monster level to 83; past that, health and damage scale exponentially from about depth 600 to 1,500, then linearly to 6,000, while rewards cap at 1,500. Azurite upkeep becomes "impossible to keep up with" around depth 1,300 (poe-vault). It pays fossils, the targeted crafting material. Praised as relaxing and a pure build test; criticised for low-depth tedium and deep walls.

**Expedition.** Explosives unearth monsters and chests. Remnants carry an upside and a downside for everything unearthed after them. Four vendors take different artefact currencies: Tujen haggles, Gwennen gambles, Rog crafts with semi-random choices, and Dannig exchanges. Risk is shown before you choose it.

**Build depth [unconfirmed details].** A skill gem is modified by support gems in linked sockets, which makes the link count a gear axis. PoE2 moves supports into the skill gem itself. Ascendancy adds 8 points from the Labyrinth (PoE1) or the Trials (PoE2).

**Criticised.** Opacity and reliance on third-party tools; power creep followed by large "manifesto" nerfs; Harvest's removal; a 13-week churn that pays off only if you play every league.

### Last Epoch (1.0, 2024)

**Loop.** Run the Monolith of Fate's echo web for loot and Corruption, push Empowered timelines, and craft Exalted bases into Legendaries in the Temporal Sanctum. The faction you joined shapes how you get items.

**Crafting with Forging Potential (FP) (Maxroll).** You add or upgrade affixes below Tier 5 (T6 and T7 only drop, on Exalted items), each tier step needing one more shard of that affix. Every craft costs FP, a random amount from a range set by the tier (T3→T4 costs 1–18); at 0 the item is finished. Glyph of Hope gives a 25% chance of no FP cost; Chaos changes the affix type; Order keeps the roll range; Despair can seal an affix into a fifth slot. Runes remove, refine (reroll values within tiers), shatter (item into shards) or duplicate (zeroing FP on both). A critical success is free and upgrades another affix. *Why it works:* you choose *what*, so direction is deterministic; randomness sets *how far*. A dropped base with high FP is exciting, and crafting never becomes Harvest's "anything, anytime".

**Legendary Potential.** A Unique with LP 1–4 and an Exalted item of the same base with 4 affixes combine at the Temporal Sanctum's Eternity Cache, once a run; LP n moves n affixes. Tier 2+ lets you guarantee one; the rest are equally likely (Maxroll). LP odds rise with area level and Corruption, doubled at Circle of Fortune rank 8. The chase, "a Unique with LP 4 and an Exalted T7 base", is two independent jackpots with a clear goal.

**Monolith of Fate.** Echoes sit on a web, each showing *its reward type before you run it*; a Beacon reveals a wide ring, and a Vessel of Chaos rerolls every unrun echo's reward. Corruption caps at 50 on normal timelines. Empowered ones start at 100 and scale without limit, raising monster health and damage along with item rarity, Exalted and Unique odds and Stability gain. Some Uniques need 50, 150 or 200 Corruption to drop, and 200 adds a fifth Blessing choice. Gaze of Orobyss, from Empowered bosses, is consumed (up to 4) for bonus Corruption; dying costs 1. Blessings chosen from bosses persist everywhere.

**Factions: trade or self-found.** One per character; switching is effectively a reset. Merchant's Guild players find *fewer* items but buy specific ones "more smoothly and deterministically". Circle of Fortune gets more and better drops plus Prophecies: pick a reward, Favor fills it, killing a named target type delivers it. Circle ranks: 1, 35% chance of double drops; 4, a set item drops as the whole set; 6, affixes 50% likelier Exalted; 8, Uniques twice as likely to have LP; 9, T7 affixes twice as common; 11, rares 25% Exalted; 12, prophecies pay double. EHG's framing: balance for choice, not optimality.

**Loot filter.** Built in: up to 75 rules, top to bottom, on affix (minimum count and tier), class, level band, rarity and type; show, hide, recolour, emphasise.

**Criticised.** FP variance bricks items late. Prophecies feel slow. The echo web becomes repetitive at high Corruption. Faction lock-in confuses players.

### Grim Dawn (2016 →)

**Loop.** Play through three difficulties with two masteries, restoring shrines for Devotion and earning faction reputation for vendor blueprints and Augments. The endgame is Crucible, Shattered Realm and Nemesis hunts.

**Dual classing.** Two of nine masteries make 36 pairs (more with the expansions). Each mastery's skill bar also raises attributes, so splitting points between two trees is a real choice.

**Devotion (official guide and Crate's post).** Each restored shrine gives 1 point, to 50 (55 with Ashes of Malmouth). The launch sky held 79 constellations and 425 stars, so a character fills about 12%. Constellations need Ascendant, Chaos, Eldritch, Order or Primordial affinity, which is *never spent*: it comes from completing other constellations or Crossroads stars, and the strongest grant none. Celestial powers bind to a skill of the right kind (attack triggers on attacks, damage-taken triggers on buffs) and gain experience *only while bound and used*, to level 20/15/10 by tier (+5 with the expansion). Points refund for a fee and an Aether Crystal, unless another constellation needs their affinity. Crate: "you cannot simply pick and choose the stars you want"; "every Devotion point will be critical". *Why it works:* spending order is the puzzle (bank affinity in cheap constellations, refund them once a capstone sustains itself), and powers that level with use reward commitment.

**Factions (official guide).** Respected opens bounties, Honored sells Writs that speed reputation, Revered sells Mandates and the best Augments and blueprints. Slaying a hostile faction escalates its spawns to heroes, then Nemesis bosses whose chests have "a very high chance" of an Epic item. A slow, deterministic route to target gear beside random drops.

**Criticised.** The constellation screen is busy. Shrine hunting is busywork on a second character. The endgame is a grind for a few blueprint components.

### Torchlight I and II [section unconfirmed; from memory]

**Loop.** A brisk, readable ARPG with a pet that carries loot to town. Torchlight I ends in the endless Shadowvault; Torchlight II in the Mapworks, maps bought with stated modifiers.

**Signature mechanics.** The Enchanter adds a random enchantment for gold, dearer with each enchant on the item, and a Disenchanter strips them: a simple, readable sink. Gems and Embers socket and can be removed at a cost. Fish transform the pet for a while. Torchlight I's Retirement passes an heirloom item, with extra enchantment headroom, to a new character, so progress persists across characters. **Criticised:** a shallow endgame and weak build depth beside PoE and Grim Dawn.

### Minecraft Dungeons (2020)

**Loop.** Short missions on a chosen difficulty and threat level. Gear arrives with enchantment *options*, and the player spends enchantment points on them.

**Enchanting (Minecraft Wiki).** Up to 3 slots a piece, each offering up to 3 random choices; you take one per slot. Tiers cost 1/2/3 points (common), 2/3/4 (powerful or gilded), 3/4/5 (gilded powerful). Salvaging or upgrading refunds *all* points; the Enchantsmith refunds one. Points come from levels (about 1 a level **[unconfirmed]**). *Why it works:* points are a budget, not a currency, so experimenting costs nothing; random options make the drop interesting, refunds keep a bad choice from being permanent.

**Difficulty.** Default, Adventure and Apocalypse, each with 7 threat levels, then Apocalypse Plus +1 to +25 (recommended power 1 → 103 → 241). A slider *defaults to a recommendation* from your power and can be set per mission; higher threat raises the gear power floor and ceiling, and some gear drops only at Adventure or above. **Criticised:** power inflation, little build depth, the same missions at bigger numbers.

## 2. Cross-cutting principles

1. **A drop is exciting when it can change how you play, not only how hard you hit.** Runewords give Teleport; Loot 2.0 put skill-changing powers on legendaries; Game Developer calls primary-stat-only loot a "false choice"; D4 admitted its uniques were "not exciting enough". Every loved game here has a tier of item that rewrites a rule.

2. **Fewer, more legible drops beat volume.** Loot 2.0's "Less is More" and D4 Season 4 both moved complexity *out of the drop* and *into crafting*; Last Epoch's filter and Mosqueira's "vacuum stuff up… terrible" make the same point.

3. **Deterministic floor, random ceiling.** Players reward determinism (Harvest's popularity, D4 Season 11's choose-your-temper), but pure determinism goes flat: Ion Hazzikostas (WoW) called removing randomness "like activating a cheat code" that loses allure, and D4's team says Season 11 "may have removed too much" hype. The stable answers choose *what* and randomise *how far* (Last Epoch's FP), extract at max (Kanai), or fix the direction and randomise the pace (gem levelling). In single player the trade-economy argument for randomness is gone; the allure argument remains.

4. **Never brick by surprise; make the last gamble opt-in and final.** D4 Season 4's tempering bricks were hated and reversed. The Season 11 Capstone rerolls without losing Quality. Minecraft Dungeons refunds every point. The accepted gambles (Sanctification, Vaal corruption) are chosen at the end and lock the item.

5. **Lasting currency sinks are crafting attempts, not taxes.** PoE's "each currency item is its own sink" works because every orb is a pull on something you want. Gold sinks that are taxes (repairs, death fees) are what GGG designed out. D4's 10M-gold Capstone reroll and D2's Stones of Jordan feeding Diablo Clone are sinks that buy variance or spectacle.

6. **An endless ladder holds if each step feeds back into the build.** GR level pays gem ranks (with a second effect at 25); the Pit pays Masterworking materials and glyph levels; Corruption pays Exalted, Unique and LP odds and Corruption-gated Uniques; Delve pays fossils. Exponential foes (+17% health a GR level; Delve's exponential band) make a soft ceiling against linear player growth. What fails is the ladder that only pays more currency or more invisible increments: the Paragon treadmill, Delve's upkeep past depth 1,300.

7. **Target farming pairs a broad random pool with a slower, certain route.** D3 has Kanai's Hope of Cain (choose the slot), Kadala (choose the slot) and Haedrig's Gift (a free set each season). D4 has boss ladders and Resplendent Sparks. Last Epoch has Prophecies and echo reward icons. Grim Dawn has faction vendors. D2 picks the boss whose drop table holds the item. The certain route is always *slower* than a lucky drop and *never* zero.

8. **Risk for reward works when it is opt-in, priced up front and local to one run.** PoE map mods show their quantity and rarity totals first; so do D4's sigils, compasses and Infernal offers, Last Epoch's Corruption, Minecraft Dungeons' threat slider and the choice of GR level. None makes the player guess what a risk pays. None shows the odds of surviving it, either; that is the gap ARISE's brief fills.

9. **Build diversity comes from constrained slots plus horizontal effects.** Kanai has three slots, one per category. Devotion fills about 12% of the sky. D4 Season 11 allows one tempered affix, and Last Epoch four affixes plus a seal. Diversity collapses when one *multiplicative* source stacks without limit (D3 sets). D4's leads say they prefer buffing the weak over nerfing the strong (Shely: "prioritize improving choice").

10. **Rotation is why players re-climb.** PoE leagues, D3 and D4 seasons and Last Epoch cycles change what is best and reset the ladder, so the same systems feel new. Offline, that has to come from rotating rules rather than wiped characters.

## 3. Ideas for ARISE, ranked

**Where ARISE starts.** Each of the 538 designs fixes its properties (crit chance, status chance, defence, resistance, accuracy, evasion, status save); only quality and magnitudes roll. The 16 sets pay flat stat bonuses (`data/sets.ts`). Upgrades step a piece 10% of its range toward perfect (`UPGRADE.share`), then 5% past it per Ascension level opened (`OVERCHARGE`). Nothing on a piece changes a rule of the fight. Every proposal keeps the fight deterministic for a seed and a list of orders, keeps the brief honest and pays only in `raid.finish`. One constraint runs through them: Android sells shard packs, so **any shard sink that buys power is power for sale**. A sink that raises power should be capped or priced in something earned only by play (Ember, trophies).

**1. Gate Seals: risk you choose, for a reward you choose (medium).** *PoE map mods with totals shown first; D4 sigils and the Infernal compass; Last Epoch Corruption; Minecraft Dungeons' threat slider.*
- **What:** each board Gate offers up to three Seals drawn from its map seed, stable for the day, each pairing a rule with a reward *kind*: *Swollen* (foes +25% HP, +40% alloy), *Roused Warden* (the Gatekeeper walks the road; the vault always leaves a blueprint), *Dim* (no rest; +1 part a fight), *Dry* (no potions; Familiarity ×2), *Ember-touched* (Ember as if one Ascension level up). Toggling Seals on the brief moves the clear ring and the "What it pays" cells together.
- **What it fixes:** finding #1 between C-Rank and S-Rank, where an invested hunter meets nothing hard and Ascension is deliberately closed. It also makes the board a target-farming tool: seal for the material you track (`s.tracked`).
- **Constraints:** Seals reuse rules the engine already speaks (Thin Rest, Short-Handed, foe multipliers), written to the run as `run.seals` the way `run.ascension` is. The brief plays the real engine, so the odds stay honest; cache its 24 simulations per Seal set. Calibration plays Seal-less runs and needs nothing, as with the Chosen; rerun the campaign with Seals taken at 75% or better.

**2. Engravings: powers that rewrite a rule, kept in a Codex (large).** *D3 legendary powers and Kanai's Cube; D4 Aspects and the Codex of Power; Grim Dawn's celestial powers.*
- **What:** 24–36 rules, not percentages: "Guard Weakens the foe whose intent targets you"; "A Health Potion also Veils you for a turn"; "Your first skill each chamber starts off cooldown"; "Shadows on Focus orders strike your Marked foe first".
- **Where they come from:** each boss family or Gatekeeper type leaves its own, at stated odds, on pieces found whole, masterworks and vaults, and the board names which. Recycling an engraved piece moves the power into a permanent Codex *at full strength* (Kanai's rule). One goes on any forged piece in each of three groups (weapon or off-hand, armour, jewellery), so at most three are live, and swapping is free (Minecraft Dungeons' refund).
- **What it adds:** drops become capabilities, and Guard and Potion orders gain consequences, which deepens the early turns the known weaknesses call shallow.
- **Constraints:** Engravings reach the fight only through `raid.heroSpec` as `UnitSpec.passives`, which already carries tree passives (`EDGE.vamp`, `execute`, `deadeye`, `thorns`), with triggers on the fight's seeded rolls. A power that changes an order's value means updating `choose()` and rechecking the intent test. `typicalHunter` carries none or one and `keptUpHunter` the System's three (an `autoEngrave`, like `autoLearn`); recalibrate. No "+X% damage" Engravings, so they cannot repeat D3's set multipliers.

**3. Rewards visible on the plan (small).** *Last Epoch's echo web (reward type shown first; Beacons; Vessel of Chaos); PoE Heist reveals.* Once the fog lifts on a cache or vault, show what it holds ("Gatesteel ×3 · Monster Bone"), tinted ember where it holds a tracked material. A detour becomes a priced choice of HP, potions and fatigue against a known prize, in two taps. Contents are already seeded from the run and the node, so no engine outcome changes; the System's road (`map.systemRoad`) is untouched, so calibration holds.

**4. Pacts at shrines (small–medium).** *D4 Infernal Offers; PoE Expedition remnants; Burning Aether lost on a wipe.* A shrine still offers two blessings, but sometimes one is a **Pact**: a stronger blessing, a stated bane for the rest of the Gate, and the bank ×1.25, which a wipe loses as it already does. It adds mid-run greed at a stated price, and a reason to take a turn by hand that Auto would not. Offers are drawn like events and written by `raid.choose`. Keep `raid.systemChoice` on the plain blessing so calibration and the brief's ring ("if the System walks it") are unchanged.

**5. Tempering at perfect: choose the property, roll its size, compare before you keep it (medium).** *D4 Season 11 (choose the affix); D4 Enchanting (keep or take, rising cost with a cap); the Masterwork Capstone reroll as a gold sink; Last Epoch's choose-what, randomise-how-far.*
- **What:** a perfect piece opens one Temper slot. The hunter picks a property from its kind's list; its size rolls in a range seeded from (item id, temper count). Each reroll shows **old against new, keep either**, so nothing bricks, at a cost that rises per reroll on that piece up to a cap.
- **What it adds:** hype back on top of ARISE's deterministic road to perfect, and a sink that keeps working at the top (finding #2).
- **Constraints:** seeded results, no clock. The top of the range is a ceiling, so the sink buys variance rather than endless power; price it in shards plus Ember so a shard pack cannot buy the ceiling outright. `keptUpHunter` carries a middle roll; recalibrate.

**6. Commissions: choose the target, fill it by playing, collect it from a named foe (small–medium).** *Last Epoch Prophecies; D3 Kadala and Hope of Cain; Grim Dawn faction blueprints; D4 Resplendent Sparks.* Commission one specific unknown blueprint of your grade or lower, or a material bundle. Every clear fills it by a stated amount, and once full the next Gatekeeper felled leaves it. It is certain and slower, beside the random routes (blueprints for the weakest kind, a set's home Gate at 30%, the Exchange's four a day), and a shard fee to start one is a sink with a ceiling. A counter on the save, paid in `raid.finish`; rerun the campaign.

**7. Spire Sigils: a thing that grows only by pushing (medium).** *D3 legendary gems on Greater Rifts; D4 glyphs levelled in the Pit and its tier skips.*
- **What:** one Sigil worn, carrying a rule that grows with rank. Rank rises by clearing a Spire block at or above it: D3's table without dice, +2 at ten or more floors above, +1 at or above, nothing below. A second effect opens at rank 25.
- **The skip:** the Pit's skip as a deterministic condition. A block cleared with no rest and no potion offers Climb On two blocks up.
- **A rule a week:** drawn from `seasonOf(day)`, so a different build is favoured each Monday, the offline stand-in for a league.
- **Constraints:** rank paid in `raid.finish`, the Sigil through `heroSpec`, `keptUpHunter` at a rank tied to level. Recalibrate the Spire.

**8. Bridges and bound passives in the constellation (medium).** *Grim Dawn Devotion: affinity never spent; about 12% filled; powers that level with use.* ARISE gates each Path's tiers on that Path's own points (`TIER_GATE` 0/3/8/14/20). Add stars *between* adjacent Paths that need points in both and pay hybrid passives (Bastion with Sight: "Guard Marks the foe that strikes you"). Add a passive bound to one equipped skill that grows with that skill's uses, as Familiarity grows a weapon. Spending order becomes the puzzle, and two-Path builds get a reason to exist. Passives go through `heroSpec`, and `autoLearn` needs a stated rule for bridges; recalibrate.

**9. A set a week (small).** *D3 Haedrig's Gift and the Season Journey.* Each Spire week names one of the 16 sets. Three stated deeds (fell its home boss twice, clear a Gate at your Ascension level, reach floor N) pay its three blueprints and their trophies. It pushes a build the hunter would not have assembled, against Auto Equip's lean toward the set already held (`SET_KEEP`). A date-fixed rotation paid in `raid.finish`; rerun the campaign.

**10. Relics: a jackpot with a stated pity (medium; needs #2).** *D2's high runes and Hellfire Torch; D4 Mythic uniques and Resplendent Sparks; Last Epoch's LP 4.* A few Relic designs each carry an Engraving no other piece has. Every S-grade boss and Trial win has a stated small chance at one and always leaves a Splinter; N Splinters forge the Relic of your choice. It gives the chase moment ARISE lacks once kit is perfect, without a lottery. A seeded roll in `raid.finish`; calibration excludes Relics as it excludes masterworks.

**11. Recycling rules (small).** *Last Epoch's filter.* Saved Inventory rules ("recycle forged rolls under 40% unless starred, worn or a set piece") applied from the results screen or with one button, with **New** marking only pieces that pass. A filter over `forge.recycleSome`; no fight effect.

**12. Guardrail: give infinite numbers a horizontal outlet (small).** *The D3 Paragon treadmill; the GR150 cap; Delve's runaway upkeep.* ARISE has three uncapped numeric ladders: points to about Level 150, past-perfect upgrades, and +8% per Ascension level past 10, with marks full at 20. Every five levels past 20, offer a *choice* (an Engraving slot, a Seal unlocked, a Sigil effect) rather than another percentage, and keep the Spire past floor 100 at +3% a block as the pure measuring stick.

### Where ARISE already matches or beats these games

- **The brief's simulated clear rate** is more honest than anything here: PoE shows loot totals but not danger, and Greater Rifts, the Pit and the Monolith give no odds. Keep it for every new mechanic, Seals above all.
- **Blueprints known for good, forged as often as materials allow,** match D4's Season 4 Codex (unlimited imprinting) and give Hope of Cain's targeting without the lottery. Drawing them for the kind of piece the hunter is weakest at is D3's smart loot where it matters most.
- **Weapon Familiarity** (+8% to the forge floor a rank, earned by play) beats D4 Season 4's random Masterwork crits: direction and pace both come from what you did.
- **Ascendant Ember**, earned only at Ascension and spent only past perfect, is a closed loop like PoE's Obducite from the Pit, and it cannot be bought.
- **Tracking a material and seeing which Gates leave it**, with sets made at their home Gates, is cleaner target farming than D2's Treasure Classes or PoE's opaque drop pools.
- **Banked loot lost on a wipe** already carries Infernal Hordes' and PoE2's death stakes. **Ascension**, with its Trials and walking Chosen, is a better-shaped ladder than D3's Paragon: named rules, a gate to each level, a masterwork as its trophy.
- **The risk:** ARISE's road to perfect is fully deterministic. D4's team says that removes the hype; #5 and #10 are the cheapest ways back without bricking.
