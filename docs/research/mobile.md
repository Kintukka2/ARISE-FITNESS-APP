# Phone dungeon crawlers and RPGs: what works without predatory design, and what ARISE can take

**How I checked these facts.** About 35 web searches and 30 page fetches, before the shared search budget ran out. Several primary sites refused fetches: shatteredpixel.com, every Fandom wiki, Game8, Prydwen, dotgg, Icy Veins and the TouchArcade reviews. Where I could not confirm a rule this session and am working from memory, it is marked **[unverified]**. Numbers without that mark come from a source in section 4.

---

## 1. Per game

### Solo Leveling: Arise (Netmarble, 2024). The reference for what fans of this fiction expect
**Loop.** A real-time action RPG. You play the protagonist, or a team of gacha "Hunters", through story chapters and repeatable Gates, and farm gear materials in key-gated modes. Pulls on two banners (hunters and weapons) drive power.

**Signature systems**
- **Gates.** Graded E to S. There were five types: Normal, Special, Red (the hardest), Bonus and Dungeon Break. Dungeon Breaks are a purple type that requires the protagonist and pays weapon limit-break materials. Entry originally cost one Gate Key: 10 a day, more with Reputation level (one guide gives 14 at Reputation 12). The daily refill only tops up to the cap, and extra keys cost 150 Essence Stones for 3, up to 10 times a day. After a clear, **Gate Mining** sends up to 3 "mining teams" (more for 2,000 Essence Stones each) to harvest the cleared Gate.
- **The 2025 consolidation** (Inven Global). One **Dungeon Entry Key** now covers Gates, Instance Dungeons, Encore Missions and the Trial of the Abyss: 100 a day, held to 200, or 400 with the paid subscription. There are 100 free **Sweep** tickets a day, plus 500 with the paid "Daily Growth Support", and Sweep works in all four modes. Red and Special Gates, rescanning and difficulty adjustment were removed. Gates now come in blue, purple and green, **you choose the reward up front** (gold, EXP, skill scrolls, shadow, weapon or artifact materials), and difficulty scales to the protagonist's level. The press framed it as removing "unwanted drops".
- **Instance Dungeons.** Rematches against story bosses, stronger, over tiers (sources say 8 or 10). Each tier has **Battle Missions**, optional conditions that pay a bonus. The Pocket Gamer guide notes that Auto can complete them but manual skill use makes it easier.
- **Encore Missions.** Bosses built around mechanics: elemental weakness and a **Break gauge** under the health bar. Shattering it stuns the boss, which is the damage window. Prydwen: "Failure to address these mechanics properly will likely prevent you from defeating the bosses."
- **Trial of the Abyss** (the staged ladder that took over the Cores farm). Stages 1–10 of one-on-one fights against "Shadows of Hunters". You pick the Core set before entering. The first time through only stages 3, 6 and 10 are open, and stage 10 needs about 3 million combat power.
- **Battlefield of Time.** A new boss each week, fought for clear time: one guide says "clear within 2 minutes" for all rewards. Seasons rank you against the whole server, and first-clear rewards don't repeat.
- **Weapons and artifacts** [unverified detail]. Weapons are gacha items limit-broken by duplicates ("Advancement"), with a further "Dimensional Advancement". Artifacts fill 8 slots (4 armour, 4 accessories) with 2- and 4-piece sets and random substats. Blessing Stones are chosen per fight loadout.
- **The Shadow army** [unverified detail]. Extracted shadows are summoned as the protagonist's skills and levelled with gold and Shadow Energy from Gates.

**Why it works.** It delivers the fantasy's power curve. You come back daily to a board of graded Gates, and you see visible power numbers (Combat Power) that gate content. The consolidation shows Netmarble learning what players want: fewer currencies, rewards you choose, and a skip for content already mastered.

**What players and critics criticise.** OpenCritic's top-critic average is 70. Game8's cons are "Game Quickly Becomes Repetitive" and "Extremely Intrusive Monetization". Critics tie auto-play to the grind: repetitive content makes Auto "a tempting possibility". Monetisation gates "the ability to quickly clear content", because sweeps and the key cap are sold through subscriptions. Every number above is a lever for selling time. **ARISE keeps the shape (a graded daily board, a weekly boss, a staged Trial, Battle Missions, a Shadow army) and none of the levers.** None of the names (Gate Mining, Encore, Battlefield of Time) should be reused.

### Shattered Pixel Dungeon (Evan Debenham, mobile-first, 2014 onward)
**Loop.** A classic turn-based roguelike of 26 floors in five regions with a boss every fifth floor [unverified count]. Each tap is one turn: tap a tile to walk there, tap a foe to attack, tap an item to use it. A run lasts one to a few hours, broken across many sessions [unverified].

**Signature mechanics**
- **Turn-based and saved continuously.** Leaving the app costs nothing, because the world only moves when you do [unverified: the save points themselves].
- **Daily runs** (v1.3.0, 2022). They work **offline with a local score history**. A new daily appears at midnight UTC, and there are no online leaderboards ("daily functionality will be pretty basic to start"). Custom seeds came in the same release.
- **Score.** Originally progression (capped at 78k) plus gold, doubled on a win, which allowed infinite gold-farming scores. The rework split it into progression, treasure, exploration, bosses and quests. Challenges multiply the score, stacking. In v3.1 the boss and quest scores fall when you take avoidable hits ("position well to avoid penalties").
- **Money.** "100% free… no advertisements, microtransactions, or paywalls." An optional donation (about $10 on Play) unlocks cosmetic extras only. The game is open source under GPLv3.
- **UI.** It runs in portrait. The developer planned six quickslots in mobile portrait and radial menus for controllers, and later drew the UI into the display cutout.

**Why it works.** Debenham: the game is for "those who like difficult games". There are no permanent power-ups, so "success depends on player skill". Turn-based play makes interruption free. That is why a game this deep works in a pocket.

**Criticism.** Mis-taps in portrait ("misinputs are possible if the player isn't paying close attention"). The Champion's action button can't be moved or hidden. The late game "feels like a chore".

### Archero (Habby, 2019) and Archero 2 (2025)
**Loop.** A one-thumb action roguelite: drag anywhere to move, and the hero fires automatically at the nearest foe whenever you stop. You clear rooms in a 50-room chapter and level up within the run. Gear and talents persist.

**Signature mechanics** (Deconstructor of Fun, guides)
- **Two states, one thumb.** "Move and Shoot." The joystick spawns where the thumb lands.
- **Pick 1 of 3 on each level-up.** The pool holds about 50 abilities and a run caps at level 11, so you never see them all. A chapter has "5 skill moments and 4 luck moments".
- **The angel and the devil.** The angel offers a heal or an ability. The devil appears **only if you took no damage from the boss** and sells a strong ability for a share of maximum HP (one source says 20%). Otherwise you spin a wheel.
- **Energy.** Each run costs 5. You regain 1 every 12 minutes (about 4 hours from empty). An ad gives 5, up to 4 a day, and 100 gems buy a full refill of 20. You get 5 back for finishing a chapter.
- **Archero 2.** Same loop, faster, with new modes (Boss Seal Battle, Trial Tower, Gold Cave). It earns about $27M a month, and lifetime revenue per download rose from about $4 to over $11.

**Why it works.** Total legibility: one input, and a new skill choice roughly every couple of minutes. The devil's no-damage condition is the best idea here: it pays for skill inside the run with a power spike, not with currency.

**Criticism.** The analysts describe a meta "added in a short soft-launch window": talents are random so "players can't pick what to upgrade", chests give one item with no guaranteed upgrade ("LTV poison"), and unwanted items can't be converted. Players report "difficulty too artificial" at later chapters, and energy cuts sessions short.

### Soul Knight (ChillyRoom)
**Loop.** A real-time twin-stick dungeon shooter [unverified structure: three worlds of five floors, 1-1 to 3-5, a boss on each fifth]. A run takes roughly 20–30 minutes [unverified].

**Signature rule.** Between some floors you **choose 1 of 3 buffs**: after 1-1, 1-3, 1-5, 2-3 and 2-5. A buff you have taken never appears again in that run, and the newest buff can be undone with the Scammer or an Amnesia Potion.

**Why it works.** The run fits a commute, and the build forms in five clearly spaced decisions.

**Criticism** (aggregators, low confidence). Some offline modes reportedly need a connection.

### Pocket Rogues (Ethereal Games)
**Loop.** A real-time top-down roguelite. You raid endless dungeons and spend between runs on a guild fortress that unlocks classes and power.

**Notes.** It runs a "dozens of hours" meta-layer. The median playtime is about 4 hours. Reviewers praise "concise and fluid" combat and the absence of ads, and complain that it gets repetitive once every dungeon is open.

**Takeaway.** A fortress meta makes failure productive, but its content is thin compared with the runs. ARISE's Forge and Shadows already do this job.

### Slay the Spire (Mega Crit; mobile 2020–21)
**Loop.** A turn-based deckbuilder climbing three acts on a branching map. Every foe shows its intent (attack and its damage number, buff, debuff).

**What the port changed, and where it went wrong.** TouchArcade found the touch targets "don't seem to have changed much if at all" from the Switch version, small on an iPhone 11. 148apps: "extremely easy to accidentally play cards when trying to inspect them", and "the cards sit a little too low". Inspecting and acting were the same gesture. Its strengths on mobile are "simple controls, a slow pace": ideal on the go. Saving and quitting mid-combat restarts that combat from its first turn [unverified on mobile].

**Why the design works.** Intents with exact numbers turn each turn into a solvable puzzle. ARISE's foe intents come from the same idea.

### Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook; iPad 2017, phones later)
**Loop.** Turn-based party expeditions through four-rank corridors, with stress and afflictions, and a hamlet that heals and upgrades between runs.

**Port lessons.** "The interface was never adjusted for smaller screens." Debuff icons were "about 2mm tall", readable only with "a very fine stylus". Hover tooltips have no touch equivalent. Menus had ordering traps: to equip an item, the character sheet must already be open. Yet several reviewers found touch "less intimidating" than a controller.

**Design intent.** Bourassa and Sigman wanted to "toy with player agency", and kept heart attacks and corpses despite Early Access complaints. Radiant mode (faster levelling, a shorter campaign) and Stygian mode (a week limit and a death cap) exist [unverified details].

### Loop Hero (Four Quarters; mobile by Playdigious, 30 April 2024)
**Loop.** The hero walks a loop automatically and fights on his own. You place tiles drawn from loot to shape the loop, then return to camp to build.

**Signature rule: graduated retreat.** Dying, or abandoning mid-battle, **loses 70%** of the expedition's resources. Leaving outside combat away from the campfire brings back **40% less**. Leaving from the campfire keeps everything.

**Port.** It is free to start with a one-time $6.99 unlock, a "revamped interface for mobile", and no portrait mode. A small touch fix players singled out: arrows show where the card is "under your finger". The touch targets were judged a little small on an iPhone 15 Pro.

**Why it works.** The retreat ladder turns every lap into a stated push-your-luck decision. Auto-combat coexists with full agency because the decisions live outside the fight.

### Wildfrost (Deadpan / Chucklefish; mobile 11 April 2024)
**Loop.** A turn-based deckbuilder in which every unit shows a **countdown counter** to its next attack. Fights are about timing.

**Port.** Tap a card to read it, drag it to play. The touch scheme is "forgiving of imprecise taps", with a two-finger tap for extra info. Version 1.1.5 made **cancelling a run require a short hold "to avoid accidental cancels"**, and 1.1.3 improved "home bar handling to avoid accidental minimizes". The model is try-before-you-buy: the first boss is free and the full game is a one-time $6.99, with no microtransactions.

**Why it works.** Counters are intents with a clock, and reading and acting are separate gestures.

### Balatro (LocalThunk / Playstack; mobile 26 September 2024)
**Loop.** Poker-hand scoring roguelike: beat blinds, buy jokers. Turn-based and fully interruptible.

**Port.** "Remastered touch controls": drags and taps instead of key shortcuts, light haptics on card taps, and an instant boot. Engadget calls it an "almost perfect" port and finds that moving cards with a finger is "inherently satisfying". The faults: no portrait mode at launch, small text on small phones, and a **Skip button in pack opening that is easy to hit by accident**, which cost the reviewer jokers.

**Takeaway.** Destructive or irreversible buttons must not sit where a hurried thumb lands.

### Arknights: Integrated Strategies (Hypergryph; permanent roguelike mode)
**Loop.** Tower-defence fights on a branching six-floor map. You recruit operators you already own **inside the run**, with a scarce currency, and collect relic-like Collectibles.

**Signature rules** (arknights.wiki.gg)
- It costs no Sanity (the game's stamina). You start with 6 Hope. Recruiting a 6★ costs 6 Hope and promoting it 3; 5★ costs 2 and 1, 4★ is free and 1, 3★ and below are free. Recruits arrive capped at Elite 1 Lv 60 with skill level 7, or at your own roster's level if that is lower. A temporary recruit costs no Hope to recruit, only to promote.
- **Life Points** are the run's lives. Each enemy that leaks costs LP, and a clear with no loss counts as "perfect". Floors 1–5 each end at a fixed node. Floor 6 and the special floors open only under conditions, and there are 3–5 endings per theme. **Endings 3–5 carry severe handicaps.**
- Difficulty runs 1–15 (18 in expansions). Older themes gave Hard two starting Collectibles, **one of them negative**, for 20% more reward.
- **The Operation Reward:** if your last run cleared at least two floors, win or lose, the next starts with a choice of 3 bonuses.
- **Monthly Contracts:** four a month, worth 500, 250, 125 and 125 (1,000 total), each refreshable up to four times a month.
- **Deep Exploration:** stated rule-changing runs with a mission objective. Meeting it marks the run "Investigation Completed" whatever the outcome.
- **Seeded runs** exist but count for no medals.
- **Interruption:** a run can be paused to the menu, but "disconnect or forced shutdown during an operation counts as a mission failure."

**Why it works.** Kotaku: the mode "forced me to use the free gacha characters I had long retired". Recruiting from your own roster under scarcity makes breadth matter. In Japan, average daily play rose 1.3× to 72.7 minutes when it launched. A run takes about an hour.

**Criticism.** It "can be brutal": bad map and roster luck can't be outplayed. Runs are long for a phone, and losing a run to a disconnect is hostile.

### Honkai: Star Rail: Simulated Universe and Divergent Universe (HoYoverse)
**Loop.** A turn-based roguelike mode. You cross a World of randomly drawn Domains with a four-character team, collecting run-only **Blessings** grouped by Path.

**Signature rules**
- **Domains:** Combat, Elite, Boss, Occurrence (an event) and Respite (shop and upgrades), plus Divergence choices. **Every combat offers 1 of 3 Blessings**, each belonging to one of the Aeons' Paths and graded by rarity. You upgrade them with Cosmic Fragments at Respites. A permanent Ability Tree persists between runs.
- **Path Resonance** [unverified numbers]: about three Blessings of one Path unlock that Path's Resonance, a strong run-only ultimate effect, which steers you to commit to one Path. Curios are run-only relics. Occurrences are text events.
- **Divergent Universe** (2.3, 19 June 2024). **Equations** activate only once you hold enough Blessings from specified Paths. Weighted Curios. You can keep a finished build and **farm Planar Ornaments from that saved build repeatedly**. Characters below the mode's Equilibrium level are raised to it.
- **The week:** points reset every Monday, and **a failed World still pays some points**. First clears of each World pay once. Gear rewards are claimed with Trailblaze Power, the stamina [unverified: 40 TP per claim, about 14,000 points to cap the week].
- Combat has **Auto and speed-up**. The game's overall stamina cap and regeneration [unverified: 240 TP, one per 6 minutes] are the predatory lever. The roguelike itself is free to enter.

**Why it works.** Blessings by Path plus Resonance make each run a build with a thesis. Equations reward reading the pool. Partial weekly points mean a bad run still counts.

**Criticism** [unverified]. Players call the weekly Simulated Universe a chore once the build is solved. The saved-build farm is HoYoverse's own answer: skip the solved part.

### Genshin Impact: Spiral Abyss and Imaginarium Theater
**Spiral Abyss** [unverified details]. Floors 9–12 rotate, with three chambers a floor and two halves a chamber, so you need **two teams of four**. Stars come from time remaining. Each cycle publishes a **Blessing of the Abyss Moon** and **Ley Line Disorders**, stated modifiers that favour certain kits. It reset twice a month, monthly since mid-2024.

**Imaginarium Theater** [unverified details]. Monthly. Only that month's three elements plus named "opening characters" may enter, and borrowed trial or supporting characters fill gaps. It is a series of acts in which fallen characters need limited revives ("Fantasia Flowers"), with buff picks between acts. It tests **roster breadth** rather than one team's peak.

**Why it works.** Stated, rotating constraints make a growing roster matter and change which builds are good each cycle, without new content. Real-time timers do not transfer to ARISE. The constraints and the two-team rule do.

### Grim Quest, Hero Siege, Eternium
- **Grim Quest** [unverified]. A text-heavy Darkest-Dungeon-like turn-based crawler for phones: party, sanity, permadeath, premium-leaning. Its lesson: turn-based menus and text suit a phone better than a ported PC UI does.
- **Hero Siege** [unverified]. A Diablo-like action roguelite with seasons. The mobile version gets little attention, and real-time twin-stick play is the opposite of ARISE.
- **Eternium** (Making Fun). A Diablo-like with **"swipe to cast" controls**: spells cast by drawing symbols. It plays offline once downloaded. "There is no limiting stamina or energy", with "no paywalls, never pay to win", and more than 90% of players never pay. It is rated 4.8 from 64K ratings. One review reports dungeon keys at about one per 8 hours. Its lesson: a touch-native input (gestures) beat a wall of buttons, and fair monetisation earned lasting goodwill.

### Idle RPGs and AFK Arena's Arcane Labyrinth
- **Offline progress** [unverified: AFK Arena's idle rewards fill for about 12 hours]. The cap turns idle play into an appointment: "log in before it's full".
- **Arcane Labyrinth** [unverified details]. Floors of fights in which **HP and energy carry over** between fights, **choose 1 of 3 relics** after each, and dead heroes stay dead. It resets every few days. **Labyrinth Sweep**: after you clear the hardest version once, later cycles can be swept for their rewards. AFK Arena earned nearly $1 billion, with gacha behind it.
- **Why the Labyrinth works.** Attrition plus relic picks make a sequence of auto-battles strategic. Sweep admits that repeating mastered content is a chore.

## 2. Cross-cutting principles

1. **The phone interrupts, so the run must survive it.** SPD, Balatro and Slay the Spire are turn-based and save continuously. Arknights IS counts a disconnect mid-operation as failure, and players hate it. The rule is that closing the app costs nothing and gains nothing.
2. **One decision every couple of minutes, from a short list.** Archero picks 1 of 3 per level, Soul Knight 1 of 3 at five fixed beats, HSR 1 of 3 per combat, AFK 1 of 3 relics per fight. Three is the number, and the beat is regular. Choices come at the gaps between fights, which are also where a phone player pauses.
3. **Run-only builds with a thesis.** HSR's Paths with Resonance and Equations, and IS's Collectible combos, turn a stack of numbers into a plan: commit to a Path, then chase the piece that completes it.
4. **Separate reading from committing.** Slay the Spire failed here and Wildfrost and Balatro succeeded. Tap or hold to read, drag or a second tap to act, and put irreversible actions behind a hold: Wildfrost's run-cancel, and Balatro's Skip, which needed one. Hover tooltips (Darkest Dungeon, and any `title=` attribute) do not exist on touch.
5. **Size and reach.** Android asks for 48dp targets 8dp apart. Hoober's street study of 1,333 phone users found 49% one-handed, 36% cradled and 15% two-handed. His later correction: people tap "the centre of the screen. More, more quickly, more accurately", not the corners. Archero's joystick-anywhere comes from the same observation.
6. **Auto that coexists with skill gives the hard content mechanics Auto doesn't solve.** SLA's Encore bosses (Break gauges, weaknesses), the Archero devil (no damage taken) and HSR's endgame all keep Auto for farming and ask for hands at the top. The honest version states it rather than hiding it.
7. **Respect mastered content.** SLA's Sweep, AFK's Labyrinth Sweep, HSR's saved-build farm and IS's "Investigation Completed" all let a proven player skip. The predatory versions sell the skip.
8. **Weekly cadence with partial credit.** HSR pays points for a failed World, IS gives an Operation Reward after any run that cleared two floors, and SPD's daily is one shared seed. The decent versions never punish a missed day. The predatory ones cap storage (SLA's 200 keys, AFK's 12-hour idle cap, Archero's energy) so that absence wastes value.
9. **Stated, rotating constraints are cheap variety.** Genshin's Moon Blessings and Ley Lines, Imaginarium's element rotation, IS's Deep Explorations and daily seeds all change which build is good without new monsters.
10. **The respectful business model is a demo and one unlock.** Wildfrost (first boss free, $6.99), Loop Hero (free to start, $6.99) and SPD (free, cosmetic donation) all work this way. Eternium shows that even free-to-play keeps goodwill without energy.

## 3. Ideas for ARISE, ranked

**What ARISE already does as well as these games or better:**
- **It resumes on the exact turn**, by replaying saved orders. That beats Slay the Spire, which restarts the combat, and Arknights IS, where a disconnect fails the run.
- **The brief's simulated clear rate** is more honest odds than any game here shows. Darkest Dungeon and IS show none.
- **Foe intents as promises:** Slay the Spire's intents and Wildfrost's counters, held to stricter rules.
- **No stamina:** fatigue fades, and there is no cap you lose value against. Compare Archero's energy, SLA's key cap and AFK's idle cap.
- **Hold-to-confirm** on extraction, promotion and rank-up, which Wildfrost added after the fact.
- **Haptics**, and pace settings (1×, 2× and Skip) in the arena.
- **A radial bag.**
- **Bosses fought by hand** at higher Ascension levels.
- **A Spire that starts at your own grade's first floor, not floor 1.**
- **One opt-in notification**, requested from a tap.
- **Daily Quests that lapse without penalty.**
- **A Licence model** that matches Wildfrost's and Loop Hero's.

**The one gap in that list is the shard packs.** They are the only item ARISE sells that buys progress. Shards pile up unused at the top, so the packs only accelerate the early climb, and none of the respectful games above sell a consumable. That is worth a deliberate decision.

### 1. A Break gauge on Gatekeepers and bosses
- **From:** SLA's Encore Missions (shatter the Break gauge to stun the boss) and HSR's weakness break.
- **What to build.**
  - Bosses and Gatekeepers carry Poise pips, more with each grade.
  - A hit removes one pip. A skill of the foe's stated weak kind (each creature family weak to one Path's kind, printed on its card) removes two.
  - At zero the foe is Broken. Its shown intent is cancelled, as a stun, takes stated extra damage until its next turn, then the pips refill.
- **Plugs into:** `core/combat.ts` unit statuses, the foe data and the foe card.
- **The decision it adds:**
  - Spend the cooldown skill now to break the boss before its telegraphed big hit, or hold it for the damage window.
  - Guard stops being a guess, because you can cancel the lethal intent instead.
  - It directly answers "early turns are shallow" and "Auto carries most play".
- **Constraints.**
  - It is deterministic.
  - A break is a stun, so the intent promise holds under the existing rule.
  - The auto policy gets a simple rule: break when the next intent would cost the hunter more than a stated share of HP, otherwise do damage. The brief plays that rule, and a player who plans ahead beats it, which is the stated aim ("Playing well beats the verdict").
  - Full recalibration of the Gates and the Spire. The Guide quotes the numbers from constants.
- **Cost:** large.

### 2. Run blessings by Path, with Resonance
- **From:** HSR's Paths, Resonance and Equations; Archero's and Soul Knight's 1-of-3 picks.
- **What to build.**
  - Replace the five flat shrine blessings (`data/nodes.ts`: Iron, Edge, Haste, Ward, Sight) with about 30 run-only blessings, six for each tree Path (Ruin, Bastion, Tempest, Arcana, Sight).
  - Some change a verb, not just a number: Guard that counters, a first turn that is always yours, potions that cleanse.
  - Shrines offer 1 of 3. A pick is also offered at each staircase down and after a Gatekeeper falls.
  - Three blessings of one Path in a run light its Resonance, a stated, stronger run-only effect.
- **Plugs into:** shrines, the run's `blessings`, and `raid.heroSpec`.
- **The decision it adds:** commit to a Path for Resonance or take the best number now. A 12-room E-Rank Gate becomes a small build, not a stat check.
- **Constraints.**
  - Offers are seeded from the run and the node, and a pick is written through `raid.choose` before it plays.
  - The System's choice is deterministic: its class's Path first, then the first option.
  - Recalibrate, and run `tests/voice.test.ts` on the names.
- **Cost:** medium.

### 3. Feats: three stated objectives on every Gate, with the odds shown
- **From:** SLA's Instance-Dungeon Battle Missions, IS's "perfect" (no LP lost), the Archero devil (no damage from the boss), Genshin's stars.
- **What to build.**
  - The board shows three Feats per Gate, drawn from its seed, such as:
    - "the boss falls with no ally down"
    - "no Health Potion drunk"
    - "the Gatekeeper falls in four rounds"
  - Each pays a stated bonus in `raid.finish`.
  - **The brief reports how often the System met each Feat in its 24 projections.** That is honest odds for skill objectives, which no game here offers.
- **Plugs into:** the Gate board, the brief, `raid.finish`, and the Daily Quest machinery for checks.
- **The decision it adds:** gives the 97%-clear hunter a reason to play by hand, and teaches play by naming good outcomes.
- **Constraints.** Every check reads the run log, so it is deterministic. Clear odds are untouched. Rerun the campaign for the pay. Keep the pay modest so Feats never become an obligation.
- **Cost:** small to medium.

### 4. Optional Pacts below S-Rank
- **From:** IS's Hard mode (a negative Collectible for +20% reward), the Archero devil, SLA's old difficulty adjustment.
- **What to build.**
  - From D-Rank, each Gate may carry one optional Pact drawn from its seed. Use milder cousins of the Ascension rules, reusing their engine hooks: thinner rests, a foe that acts first, no potions.
  - Each Pact has stated extra pay.
  - The brief shows both clear rates, with and without the Pact.
  - The Pact is written to the run at entry, as `run.ascension` is.
- **Plugs into:** the board, the brief, the Ascension rule hooks and `raid.finish`.
- **The decision it adds:** answers weakness 1, the invested hunter who outgrows the calibration mid-climb, by letting them buy back risk, and the honest brief makes it a real wager.
- **Constraints.**
  - Deterministic and simulable.
  - Calibrate the pay so its expected value is fair at the typical hunter's clear rate.
  - Campaign rerun.
- **Cost:** medium.

### 5. Hold to read: exact damage on intents and skills, and nothing to hover
- **From:** Slay the Spire's intent numbers, the touch fixes in Wildfrost, Balatro and Loop Hero, and Darkest Dungeon's hover failure.
- **What exists now.** The arena's foe intent reads "⟶ ability · target", and its detail sits in a `title` attribute (`Arena.tsx`, `Intent`). That is a hover tooltip, which no phone can show.
- **What to build.**
  - The intent chip prints the damage range the foe will deal to its target after defence, and what Guard would bring it to.
  - Holding a skill (or a foe while a skill is armed) shows that skill's range against that foe and marks **Kills** or **May kill**.
  - Tap still acts and hold reads, so the two never share a gesture.
  - Put the target list in the action tray as well as on the foe cards at the top, so aiming stays in the thumb's zone.
- **Constraints.**
  - A pure function in `core/combat.ts` computes min and max, never the seeded roll, so a preview can't be used to scum crits.
  - It doesn't change the fight, so there is nothing to recalibrate.
- **Cost:** small to medium.

### 6. The Daily Rift: one seed for every phone
- **From:** SPD's offline daily with local score history, Wildfrost's and Slay the Spire's dailies, Balatro's seeds.
- **What to build.**
  - One extra Gate a day, seeded from the date alone, so it is the same map on every device.
  - Two stated modifiers.
  - One attempt, costing fatigue like any Gate.
  - A score from floors reached, rounds taken and Feats met, kept in a local history.
  - A fixed, stated reward.
- **Stretch: a replay code.** A run is a seed plus a list of orders, so a short code can let a friend's phone replay and **verify** a score offline. That is a leaderboard with no server, possible only because ARISE's fights are deterministic.
- **Plugs into:** `gates`, `map.mapSeed`, records and Secret Quests.
- **Constraints.** It is deterministic by construction and the brief can project it. The score is local, and builds differ, so compare against yourself first.
- **Cost:** medium, or large with replay codes.

### 7. The Shadow Gauntlet: breadth over a top squad
- **From:** Spiral Abyss's two teams, Imaginarium Theater's roster test, IS's recruiting from a roster you already own.
- **What to build.**
  - A weekly run of several chambers, beside the Spire on the Quests screen, in which **a Shadow that fought one chamber cannot be sent into another**.
  - It pays Shadow Essence and a stated prize.
- **The decision it adds:**
  - It makes the reserve and the roster's sort and filter tools matter.
  - It gives Essence and promotions a sink, which answers part of weakness 2 (currency piling up at the top).
  - It turns duplicate extractions into the argument for a broader army.
- **Constraints.**
  - The System's choice is Auto Deploy over the unused Shadows, strongest first, which is deterministic.
  - Calibrate against `keptUpHunter` with an army of stated size, which needs a fixture with breadth.
- **Cost:** medium.

### 8. Bind for the run
- **From:** IS recruiting with Hope mid-run; HSR's downloader, which adds a character in a Respite.
- **What to build.**
  - A creature lingering after a fight may be bound **for this run only**, at a stated mana cost and with no odds.
  - It fights in one extra run-only slot and dissolves at the exit, or can then be extracted at the usual odds.
- **The decision it adds:** mana spent now against extraction later. That is a real choice in short early runs, where turns are thinnest.
- **Constraints.**
  - A step choice written through `raid.choose`.
  - The System binds only when its mana after binding stays above a stated reserve.
  - Recalibrate.
- **Cost:** medium.

### 9. The Spire's Current: a stated rule each week
- **From:** Genshin's Blessing of the Abyss Moon and Ley Line Disorders.
- **What to build.** Each Spire week names one Current, seeded from the week, that favours a Path or a Shadow family. For example: "Bleed lasts a turn longer", or "Bastion skills cool a turn faster".
- **The decision it adds:** a reason to respec (the first reset is free) or swap the squad, and variety in a mode everyone climbs weekly.
- **Constraints.** Choose Currents that are symmetric enough to calibrate, or calibrate each one. They are deterministic.
- **Cost:** small to medium.

### 10. Session fit: how long a Gate takes, and a one-tap walk out
- **From:** Archero's chapter as a known unit; the lesson from all the phone ports that players plan around time.
- **What to build.**
  - The brief already walks the System's road 24 times. Record the rooms and rounds of each walk and show "about N rooms · M rounds" on the board and in the brief.
  - Add **Walk Out**: one tap that paths back to the entrance through cleared rooms, written as `raid.moveTo` steps.
- **The feeling:** you can pick a Gate that fits the bus ride, and a cut-short session ends with the haul banked rather than abandoned.
- **Constraints:** reporting and shortcuts only, so there are no rule changes.
- **Cost:** small.

### 11. Spire start from the proven floor
- **From:** AFK's Labyrinth Sweep, SLA's Sweep, IS's "Investigation Completed".
- **What to build.** On Monday, a hunter may start this week two blocks below last week's best, instead of at their grade's first floor. The skipped blocks pay nothing, and the week's settlement already pays by the highest floor.
- **The feeling:** the fatigue goes to floors that challenge, so mastered content isn't a weekly chore.
- **Constraints:** no new fight rules. Rerun the campaign for Spire pay.
- **Cost:** small.

### 12. The Heart beyond the boss
- **From:** IS's conditional sixth floor and handicapped endings; the Archero devil.
- **What to build.**
  - In B-grade Gates and above, a sealed stair beyond the boss opens if the run met a stated condition (for example, the Gatekeeper fell and no rest was used).
  - It leads to one more floor with a stronger boss and a stated prize. Everything banked is still at risk.
- **The decision it adds:** push-your-luck at the moment you know your state best.
- **Constraints.**
  - The System's road never takes it, so the clear rate stays the Gate's own. The brief adds a second line for the Heart's odds.
  - A new layout version, so older layouts draw as they did.
  - Calibrate separately.
- **Cost:** medium to large.

### 13. Reserve salvage: Gate Mining without a clock
- **From:** SLA's Gate Mining; AFK's Bounty Board.
- **What to build.** Shadows in reserve work the cleared Gate and add a stated material bonus by their number and level, settled in `raid.finish`. There is no dispatch, no timer and nothing to collect later.
- **The feeling:** the reserve has a job.
- **Caution:** pay materials, not shards, and taper the bonus. Rerun the campaign.
- **Cost:** small.

### 14. Notifications and one Daily Quest swap
- **From:** the Android 13 notification guidance; IS's contract refresh.
- **What to build.**
  - Keep the single "rested" notification. Offer it in context the first time the hunter is too tired to enter, with one line and a tap, rather than only from Settings. Android 13 and later start new installs with notifications off, and Google advises waiting "before you ask users to grant any permissions."
  - Never add streak, lapse or "come back" notifications.
  - Separately, allow one Daily Quest swap a day, written to the save so nothing redraws twice. IS allows four refreshes a month.
  - Rerun balance.
- **Cost:** small.

### Considered and rejected
- **Gesture casting (Eternium):** in a turn-based fight with no timing pressure, large tap targets beat gestures.
- **Idle rewards with a cap:** this is an appointment mechanic. ARISE's fatigue already does the useful part of it.
- **Real-time timers (Spiral Abyss stars, Battlefield of Time):** they break both determinism and the "no reflex" constraint. Rounds taken is the turn-based equivalent.
